About WhatItMeans

WhatItMeans explains what financial news means. Headlines tell you that something happened. They rarely tell you why it matters, who it reaches, or how it travels from an announcement to a price.

How every story is structured

Each item follows the same seven steps, in the same order, every time:

  1. What happened — the facts, with numbers.
  2. Who is affected — the specific groups that feel it.
  3. Which sectors — and whether it is a tailwind or a headwind.
  4. The transmission mechanism — the causal chain, step by step.
  5. Market reaction — what the source reporting says markets did.
  6. Bull and bear cases — the strongest arguments on both sides.
  7. Variables to watch — what would confirm or break each case.

The structure never changes on purpose. Once you have read a few, you know where to look for the part you care about.

Where the analysis comes from

We read public RSS feeds from Indian and global financial publishers, group articles covering the same story, and generate an explanation from those articles with automated assistance. We show the headline and a short excerpt, and always link to the original publisher. We do not reproduce full articles.

What this is not

This is not investment advice. We are not registered with SEBI as a Research Analyst or an Investment Adviser, and nothing here is a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold anything. You will never find a price target, a stop loss, or a "buy this" on this site — not because we are being coy, but because giving those is a regulated activity we are not registered for.

The bull and bear cases describe arguments that market participants are making. They are not our views, and presenting both is not a way of hinting at one.

Accuracy

Explanations are generated automatically and reviewed by automated compliance checks before publication. They can still be wrong or incomplete. Every story links its sources — if something looks off, the source is one click away, and it is the authority, not us.

Data retention

We do not keep news data. Articles and analysis are discarded within 24 hours, and the published site is rebuilt from scratch on every run. We do not use cookies, we do not run accounts, and we do not collect personal data.

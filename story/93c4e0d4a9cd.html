The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: corporate health coverage ends when an employee leaves a job
  2. step 2: families are forced to pay medical bills or buy private policies
  3. step 3: higher out‑of‑pocket spending reduces disposable income and raises demand for private insurance
  4. step 4: insurers see higher premium revenues while consumers see higher health‑care costs

When employment ends, the trigger is the termination of the employer’s health plan. This forces households to either absorb medical costs directly or seek new coverage, which typically involves paying premiums. The lag is a few weeks to months as people evaluate options, after which insurers may record higher sales and consumers feel tighter budgets. The effect shows up in insurance premium growth and potentially in reduced consumer spending elsewhere.

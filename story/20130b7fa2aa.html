India GDP rises 7.8% as modi urges curb gold buying, travel

Modi’s call to cut gold purchases comes as GDP hits 7.8%

What happened

India's gross domestic product expanded by 7.8% in the latest reporting period. On September 1, Prime Minister Narendra Modi posted a video from the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit in Bishkek urging citizens to reduce gold purchases and foreign travel. The appeal linked personal spending choices to the country's goal of becoming a developed economy by 2047.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: modi publicly urges less gold purchase and foreign travel
  2. step 2: consumers feel pressure to curb discretionary spending on gold and trips
  3. step 3: lower demand translates into reduced gold imports and foreign‑exchange outflow for travel
  4. step 4: current‑account balance improves, easing rupee pressure and supporting broader growth

The prime minister's statement acts as a behavioural nudge (step 1) that pushes households to rethink discretionary purchases (step 2). Because gold is largely imported and overseas travel consumes foreign exchange, a cut in these items directly trims import volumes and outflows (step 3). The macro effect appears in the current‑account figures and can temper rupee depreciation, which may feed back into inflation and growth outlook (step 4). The lag from consumer sentiment to import data typically spans weeks to a few months.

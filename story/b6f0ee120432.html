Indian equities end 8‑week slump as Sensex rebounds on falling crude

FII outflows of Rs 30,294 crore contrast with DII buying of Rs 30,313 crore

What happened

On Oct 10, 2026 Indian equities broke an eight‑week losing run as the Sensex and Nifty 50 posted modest gains. The rally was aided by a decline in crude oil prices, while foreign portfolio investors (FIIs) sold Rs 30,294 crore of stocks for the eighth straight week. Domestic institutional investors (DIIs) bought Rs 30,313 crore, offsetting the foreign outflow.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. step 1: crude oil prices fell
  2. step 2: lower energy costs improve corporate profit outlook
  3. step 3: investors anticipate better earnings and lower inflation pressure
  4. step 4: stock prices rise and indices recover

The price drop in crude oil reduces operating expenses for many companies, especially in energy‑intensive sectors. This improves profit forecasts, which nudges analysts to upgrade earnings estimates. Better earnings expectations lift demand for shares, pushing the Sensex and Nifty higher. The effect typically unfolds over a few weeks as data and earnings reports filter through.

Market reaction

The sources reported that the Sensex and Nifty rebounded, ending the eight‑week slide, while average daily turnover fell to a six‑month low, reflecting lingering bearish sentiment.

US yields and dollar rise as robust jobs data fuels Fed rate hike bets

Unexpected addition of 162,000 US jobs in August triggers global market shifts.

  • US economy added 162,000 jobs in August, beating analyst expectations.
  • Strong employment data increases the likelihood of a Federal Reserve interest rate hike.
  • Upcoming US consumer and producer inflation data will clarify the Fed's next move.

What happened

The US job market added 162,000 positions in August 2026, surpassing analyst expectations and demonstrating economic resilience. This strong labor data immediately boosted expectations for a Federal Reserve interest rate hike in September 2026. Consequently, US Treasury yields and the US dollar strengthened, while global stocks and gold prices declined.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. Stronger-than-expected US job growth of 162,000 positions in August
  2. Market participants expect the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates to cool the economy
  3. US Treasury yields and the US dollar rise as capital seeks higher, safer returns
  4. Foreign investors pull capital out of emerging markets like India to reinvest in US assets
  5. Indian stock indices and the rupee face downward pressure

The process begins immediately as bond markets price in a higher probability of a Fed rate hike, causing US Treasury yields to rise within minutes of the jobs report. Over the following days, a stronger US dollar pressures emerging market currencies, including the Indian rupee, as global capital flows back to the US. Within weeks, foreign portfolio investors may reduce their exposure to Indian equities, seeking the safer, higher yields available in US debt. Ultimately, this capital reallocation shows up as volatility in Indian stock indices and pressure on import costs over the quarter.

Market reaction

US stocks ended lower, with a broad selloff across major indexes. US Treasury yields and the US dollar rose, while gold prices slid to set up a weekly loss.

Global AI capex slowdown risks hurting India growth recovery, says Nuvama

A slowdown in global AI spending threatens India's domestic corporate revenue growth.

  • Nuvama warned that a global AI capex slowdown could hurt India's cyclical recovery.
  • Weaker global demand may depress Indian corporate revenues and slow down credit growth.
  • Global technology spending trends and domestic credit growth indicators are key to watch.
IT & TechBanking & Financial ServicesMetals & MiningFiscal & BudgetCredit & LiquidityGrowth

What happened

On August 22, 2026, a report by brokerage firm Nuvama warned that any slowdown in global artificial intelligence capital expenditure could disrupt India's ongoing economic recovery. The report highlighted that weaker global demand and falling commodity prices would likely pressure corporate revenues and domestic credit growth. This risk is compounded by India's currently tight monetary policy environment.

Who is affected

Indian IT services exporters
They could face lower order inflows and slower revenue growth if global tech firms cut back on AI-related capital expenditure.
Domestic corporate lenders
Banks and financial institutions may experience a deceleration in credit growth as corporate expansion plans slow down.
Commodity producers
Indian metal and materials companies could see weaker realizations and earnings due to softer global commodity prices.

Which sectors

  • IT & Tech headwind — A reduction in global AI capital expenditure directly limits the deal pipeline and contract sizes for Indian software exporters.
  • Banking & Financial Services headwind — Slower corporate revenue growth and weaker demand typically lead to lower credit utilization and slower loan book expansion.
  • Metals & Mining headwind — Weaker global demand tends to depress commodity prices, squeezing profit margins for domestic producers.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. Step 1: Global technology firms reduce their capital expenditure on artificial intelligence infrastructure.
  2. Step 2: Global demand softens, leading to a decline in international commodity prices.
  3. Step 3: Indian corporate revenues contract due to weaker export demand and lower realizations.
  4. Step 4: Slower business activity reduces the demand for new loans, dragging down domestic credit growth.

The transmission begins when global tech giants scale back their massive AI investments, which immediately impacts global tech sentiment. Within one to two quarters, this slowdown dampens broader global demand, causing commodity prices to soften. Indian exporters and commodity producers feel the pinch next as their revenues shrink over the subsequent six months. Finally, with lower revenues and high domestic interest rates, Indian corporates defer expansion, leading to a visible slowdown in bank credit growth over nine to twelve months.

Jargon, in plain words

capex
Capital expenditure, which is the money a company spends to buy, maintain, or improve its long-term physical or technological assets.
cyclical recovery
An economic turnaround driven by regular business cycles, where demand, production, and employment naturally rise after a slowdown.
credit growth
The rate at which the total amount of loans provided by banks and financial institutions to businesses and individuals increases.

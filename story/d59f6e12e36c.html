US 10-year Treasury yield nears 5% as Yes Securities downplays market risk

US Treasury yield at 4.9% triggers debate among Indian investors

What happened

The US 10-year Treasury yield climbed above 4.9% on 12 September 2026, moving toward the 5% threshold that was briefly reached in 2023. Yes Securities issued a contrarian view, arguing that the rise in global yields reflects a stronger US economy and that market fears are overblown.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: US 10-year Treasury yield approaches 5%
  2. step 2: global borrowing costs rise, making US assets more attractive
  3. step 3: capital may flow out of emerging markets like India, pressuring the rupee and lifting Indian bond yields
  4. step 4: higher bond yields increase corporate borrowing costs and can dampen equity valuations

The trigger is the US 10‑year yield nearing 5%. That forces investors to demand higher returns on non‑US assets, prompting some capital outflows from India. The outflows weaken the rupee and push up yields on Indian government bonds, which then raise the cost of corporate loans and can reduce equity multiples. The lag is typically a few weeks for capital flow shifts and a month or two for bond‑market adjustments to feed into corporate financing costs.

The bull case

What optimists are arguing

Optimists, led by Yes Securities, argue that the yield rise signals a robust US economy and that Indian markets have sufficient liquidity buffers, so the impact will be limited.

HDFC Bank and Axis Bank report Q2 profit growth as bad loans stay low

Axis Bank saw profit jump 38% while HDFC Bank gained just 5%.

  • Axis Bank and HDFC Bank reported second-quarter profit growth alongside stable asset quality.
  • Moderate net interest income growth shows lending margins face ongoing pressure across large lenders.
  • Operating cost trends and leadership transition execution at HDFC Bank in coming quarters.
Banking & FinancialsCredit & Liquidity

What happened

On October 17, 2026, HDFC Bank reported a 5% year-on-year increase in standalone net profit to ₹19,510 crore for Q2 FY27, with net interest income rising 10% to ₹34,619 crore. Axis Bank posted a 38% rise in net profit to ₹7,003 crore for the same quarter, supported by a 12% rise in net interest income to ₹15,371 crore, though higher costs caused it to miss analyst estimates. Both lenders maintained low bad-loan metrics, with gross non-performing asset ratios at 1.11% for HDFC Bank and 1.16% for Axis Bank.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. Lenders expand loan portfolios while deposit repricing sets funding costs
  2. Net interest income grows at a moderate 10% to 12% annual pace
  3. Operating overheads and credit provisions absorb a share of gross income
  4. Remaining surplus flows directly into quarterly net profit and balance-sheet equity

When banks issue new credit, interest income expands over months as loan books reprice. Simultaneously, deposits reprice with a lag of one to two quarters, determining the net interest margin earned on advances. From this gross operating profit, institutions deduct administrative and employee expenses, which Hindu BusinessLine noted pinched Axis Bank's quarter. The final balance after loan-loss provisions settles into quarterly net earnings and regulatory capital.

Jargon, in plain words

Net interest income
The difference between the total interest a bank earns from borrowers and the interest it pays to depositors.
Gross NPA
The total value of loans that borrowers have stopped repaying on schedule, expressed as a percentage of all loans.
Net interest margin
The percentage spread between interest earned on loans and interest paid on funding, relative to total interest-generating assets.

Foreign investors buy Rs 7,759 crore of Indian G-Secs in November as outflows end

Debt inflows reached Rs 7,759 crore following a two-month period of net selling.

  • FPIs bought Rs 7,759 crore of Indian government bonds in November, reversing two months of selling.
  • Foreign debt inflows help stabilize the Indian rupee and lower borrowing costs for government.
  • Future foreign capital flows and movement in global interest rate conditions.
Banking and Financial ServicesCurrency

What happened

Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) invested Rs 7,759 crore into Fully Accessible Route (FAR) Indian government securities in November 2026. This net buying comes after two consecutive months of foreign capital outflows from Indian debt. The turnaround was accompanied by a recovering Indian rupee, a rally in government bonds, and easing global financial conditions.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. step 1: Global financial conditions ease and rupee stabilizes
  2. step 2: Foreign investors purchase Rs 7,759 crore of Indian government bonds
  3. step 3: Increased demand drives up bond prices and lowers benchmark yields
  4. step 4: Lower benchmark yields reduce economy-wide borrowing costs over time

Easier global financial conditions and a recovering rupee lower currency risk for overseas buyers. This encourages foreign portfolio investors to purchase Indian government securities, pouring Rs 7,759 crore into the market in November. Over days and weeks, this influx of capital bids up bond prices, which pushes bond yields down. Because government bond yields act as the benchmark for corporate and consumer borrowing rates, lower yields eventually translate into cheaper interest rates for domestic lenders and borrowers over subsequent quarters.

Jargon, in plain words

FPI (Foreign Portfolio Investment)
Investments made by foreign individuals or funds in a country's public financial assets like stocks and bonds.
FAR G-Secs (Fully Accessible Route Government Securities)

Indian Railways plans to quadruple tracks across seven major corridors

Expanding to four parallel tracks could eventually clear passenger waitlists across four metro routes.

  • Indian Railways plans to expand seven key transit corridors into four parallel tracks.
  • Quadrupling lines aims to separate coal freight from passenger transport, reducing congestion.
  • Approvals, land acquisition timelines, and capital expenditure outlay for the quadrupling projects.
Infrastructure & EngineeringPower & UtilitiesLogistics & Freight

What happened

On November 7, 2026, Railway Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw announced plans to expand seven major railway corridors to four parallel tracks. The quadrupling project covers core routes connecting Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, and Chennai. The expansion aims to accommodate over 1,000 additional regular passenger trains while addressing severe network congestion caused by heavy freight movement, particularly coal.

Who is affected

Thermal power plants and coal producers
Faster freight movement reduces transit delays and lowers bulk fuel delivery bottlenecks.
Long-distance passenger rail travelers
Capacity expansion enables up to 1,000 additional regular daily trains, easing ticket waitlists.
Infrastructure EPC contractors
Engineering and construction companies stand to gain significant order flow from track-laying contracts.

Which sectors

  • Infrastructure & Engineering tailwind — Quadrupling seven trunk routes requires massive track construction, signaling, and electrification expenditure.
  • Power & Utilities tailwind — Dedicated freight capacity improves coal transit times to thermal generation plants, reducing fuel shortages.
  • Logistics & Freight tailwind — Higher network throughput allows railways to move higher cargo volumes without crowding passenger traffic.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. step 1: Government approves track quadrupling across seven major corridors
  2. step 2: Railway Ministry tenders contracts for track laying, earthworks, and signaling
  3. step 3: Expanded rail network separates heavy coal freight from passenger operations
  4. step 4: Freight turnaround times drop and passenger throughput rises by over 1,000 trains

The project begins with capital expenditure outlays for land acquisition, track laying, and signaling upgrades over the coming quarters. As infrastructure firms execute work over several years, line capacity across the seven key corridors will expand significantly. Once completed, separating bulk freight—especially coal shipments—from passenger traffic reduces operational bottlenecks. This dual-track model cuts freight turnaround times for industrial customers and allows Indian Railways to run over 1,000 new passenger trains without creating network congestion.

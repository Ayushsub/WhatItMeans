RBI projects private capex at ₹3.2 lakh crore for 2026‑27

Banks see ₹3.2 lakh crore of private projects slated for 2026‑27

  • RBI estimates private capex at ₹3.2 lakh crore for FY 2026‑27.
  • Higher investment could lift demand for financing and capital goods.
  • Watch credit growth and global risk cues for actual impact.
bankingcapital goodsconstructionFiscal & Budget

What happened

The Reserve Bank of India said private sector capital expenditure is projected to reach ₹3.2 lakh crore in the 2026‑27 fiscal year. Funding for these projects is expected to come from banks, financial institutions, external commercial borrowings and initial public offerings. The RBI cautioned that global uncertainties could temper sentiment toward the outlook.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. step 1: RBI projects private capex of ₹3.2 lakh crore for FY 2026‑27
  2. step 2: firms seek funding via banks, NBFCs, ECBs and IPOs
  3. step 3: increased credit supply and equity issuance raise financing activity and cost of capital
  4. step 4: banks earn higher interest margins; capital‑goods firms see higher sales; earnings reflect in quarterly results

The RBI’s estimate acts as a signal that firms will look for large amounts of financing. This pushes banks and non‑bank lenders to expand loan books while companies also tap equity markets. The lag is a few weeks for loan approvals and a quarter for the impact to show up in earnings. As financing volumes rise, interest‑income and fee income for lenders increase, and capital‑goods makers record higher sales, which appear in their quarterly reports.

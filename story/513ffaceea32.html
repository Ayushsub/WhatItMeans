Brent crude breaches $100 as Middle East risks add war premium

Higher shipping costs and $100 crude threaten India's energy import bill.

  • Brent crude traded above $100 a barrel amid Middle East supply risks.
  • Sustained high oil prices threaten to increase India's import bill and inflation.

What happened

On October 10, 2026, Brent crude traded above $100 per barrel as geopolitical risks in the Middle East sustained a war premium over West Texas Intermediate crude. Prolonged strength in crude prices threatens to raise shipping costs and strengthen the US dollar relative to Indian import requirements.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East raise supply concern risks
  2. Brent crude breaches $100 per barrel while shipping fees rise
  3. Indian refiners pay higher US dollar prices for imported crude
  4. Inflated raw material expenses pressure domestic corporate margins and consumer inflation

Geopolitical friction adds a risk premium to crude oil, driving up global benchmark prices alongside shipping expenses. Because India imports over 80% of its crude needs, domestic importers must spend more foreign exchange to secure shipments. These elevated costs reach refiners within a few weeks, which subsequently forces higher input costs onto downstream consumers and manufacturing companies over following quarters.

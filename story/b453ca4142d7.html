RBI rate-cut cycle may end with potential 50-75 bps hike by FY27

A potential 75 basis point rate hike by FY27 threatens Indian bond portfolios.

  • PGIM India expects the RBI to raise interest rates by 50-75 basis points.
  • Rising bond yields will lower existing bond prices but offer better new investment yields.
  • Watch crude oil price movements and retail inflation prints over the coming quarters.
Banking and Financial ServicesInfrastructure and Real EstateInterest RatesInflationCredit & Liquidity

What happened

Puneet Pal of PGIM India stated that India's interest rate-cut cycle is likely over, with the Reserve Bank of India potentially raising policy rates by 50 to 75 basis points by financial year 2027. This shift is driven by expectations of rising global crude oil prices and persistent, sticky domestic inflation. Consequently, bond yields are expected to trend higher.

Who is affected

investors holding long-duration debt funds
For someone holding these funds, the impact is a short-term capital loss as rising yields cause existing bond prices to fall.
corporate borrowers
For a business seeking fresh debt, borrowing costs will likely rise by 50 to 75 basis points as lenders price in the rate hikes.
target-maturity fund investors
For someone looking to lock in fixed returns, rising yields will offer opportunities to purchase new funds with higher interest payouts.

Which sectors

  • Banking and Financial Services mixed — While higher interest rates can improve net interest margins on new loans, they also lead to mark-to-market treasury losses on banks' existing bond portfolios.
  • Infrastructure and Real Estate headwind — These capital-intensive sectors face higher interest expenses on their substantial debt loads, squeezing profit margins.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. Global crude oil prices rise and domestic inflation remains sticky
  2. RBI halts rate cuts and begins raising the repo rate by up to 75 basis points
  3. Bond yields rise as investors demand higher returns to offset inflation
  4. Prices of existing fixed-rate bonds fall, while new debt issuances offer higher coupon rates

When crude oil prices rise, it increases domestic transport and manufacturing costs, which typically feeds into retail inflation within one to two quarters. To curb this sticky inflation, the RBI raises its benchmark repo rate, a process that usually transmits to commercial bank lending rates over three to six months. As policy rates rise, market yields on government and corporate bonds adjust upward almost immediately. This rise in yields causes the market value of existing bonds to fall, while simultaneously allowing new investors to lock in higher interest rates.

The bull case

What optimists are arguing

Bulls suggest that rising yields could offer favorable entry conditions for investors in high-quality corporate bonds and target-maturity funds, potentially allowing them to secure higher yields.

Depends on:

  • Yields peaking near the projected 50-75 basis point hike range
  • Corporate credit defaults remaining low during the tightening cycle

The bear case

What pessimists are arguing

Bears argue that sticky inflation and rising crude prices will force aggressive rate hikes that depress bond portfolios, increase corporate borrowing costs, and slow down economic growth.

Depends on:

  • Crude oil prices remaining elevated for an extended period
  • Domestic retail inflation staying consistently above the RBI's comfort zone

Key variables to watch

  • Global crude oil pricesongoing
    Validates the bear case of sticky inflation and rate hikes if prices stay high, or the bull case if they cool down.
  • RBI monetary policy committee announcementsongoing

Jargon, in plain words

Basis points
A financial unit of measurement equal to 1/100th of a percent (e.g., 100 basis points equals 1%).
Yield
The annual return on an investment, expressed as a percentage, based on its current market price.
Target-maturity funds
Debt mutual funds that invest in bonds matching a specific maturity date, reducing interest rate risk if held to maturity.

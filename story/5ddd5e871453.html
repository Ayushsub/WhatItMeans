India's forex reserves fall $14.9bn to $765.9bn as RBI plans swaps

Banks hold excess cash after RBI’s $140bn capital inflow

  • Forex reserves dropped sharply
  • Liquidity management could tighten markets
  • Watch RBI swap execution and rupee moves
bankingexportersCurrency

What happened

In the week ending 25 September 2026 India’s foreign exchange reserves fell by about $14.9 billion, taking the total to $765.90 billion, mainly due to a drop in foreign currency assets. The RBI announced it will conduct at least $10 billion of forex swaps to absorb excess cash after earlier measures attracted more than $140 billion of foreign capital. Earlier data from the week ending 11 September showed a smaller fall of $4.9 billion to $780.7 billion after a peak of $785 billion.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. step 1: sharp fall in foreign currency assets reduces overall reserves
  2. step 2: RBI launches $10 bn forex swaps to soak up surplus liquidity
  3. step 3: swaps shrink banks’ cash balances, tightening short‑term funding and pushing up interbank rates
  4. step 4: higher funding costs feed into corporate loan rates and compress bank net‑interest margins

The reserve drop forces the RBI to act to prevent excess liquidity from fueling inflation. By offering swaps, the central bank swaps rupee for foreign currency, effectively pulling rupees out of the banking system. This process takes a few days to weeks to reflect in interbank rates, after which banks pass higher costs to borrowers and see tighter margins in earnings. The lag between the swap announcement and observable changes in loan pricing is typically measured in weeks.

RBI may hike repo rate by 75 to 100 bps as inflation persists, says SBI Capital

Higher EMI burdens loom for retail borrowers facing a possible 100 basis point cycle.

  • SBI Capital projects RBI will raise repo rate by 75 to 100 basis points.
  • Borrowing costs for housing and auto loans will rise as banks pass on hikes.
  • Upcoming consumer inflation data releases leading up to the December 2026 policy review.
Banking & FinancialsReal EstateAutomobilesInterest RatesInflation

What happened

SBI Capital Markets projected on October 10, 2026, that the Reserve Bank of India may raise the repo rate by 75 to 100 basis points during the current monetary tightening cycle. The brokerage noted that gradual rate increases are the most likely path, though persistent inflation could force a larger hike in December 2026.

Who is affected

Floating-rate home loan borrowers
Interest rates on floating loans will increase by up to 100 basis points, raising monthly repayments or lengthening loan tenures.
Corporate borrowers
Companies seeking short-term working capital or long-term loan facilities face elevated interest costs on new borrowing.

Which sectors

  • Banking & Financials mixed — Banks may temporarily boost margins on floating-rate loans, but higher borrowing costs risk dampening overall credit demand.
  • Real Estate headwind — Rising home loan interest rates increase total cost of ownership for buyers, slowing housing sales growth.
  • Automobiles headwind — Higher auto loan financing costs reduce affordability for vehicle buyers.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: RBI increases the repo rate to curb persistent consumer inflation
  2. step 2: Commercial banks raise external benchmark lending rates for borrowers
  3. step 3: Monthly EMIs on floating-rate retail and corporate loans increase
  4. step 4: Reduced household disposable income cools consumer demand across sectors

When the Reserve Bank of India increases the repo rate, commercial banks immediately face higher costs to borrow short-term funds from the central bank. To protect their margins, banks transmission policy changes by resetting external benchmark-linked lending rates upward within days to weeks. Over subsequent months, higher debt servicing costs leave consumers with less disposable income, dampening discretionary spending across the economy.

The bull case

What optimists are arguing

Bulls argue that timely interest rate increases will anchor consumer inflation expectations and strengthen bank net interest margins without stalling overall economic growth.

Depends on:

  • Inflation moderating toward central bank targets
  • Resilient corporate credit demand despite higher borrowing costs

The bear case

What pessimists are arguing

Bears argue that a cumulative 100 basis point hike will weigh heavily on consumer spending, slow retail loan growth, and cause debt stress for leveraged households.

Depends on:

  • Consumer price inflation remaining persistently elevated
  • A slowdown in discretionary consumption and credit intake

Jargon, in plain words

Repo rate
The benchmark interest rate at which the Reserve Bank of India lends short-term funds to commercial banks.
Basis points (bps)
A unit of measure for interest rates where 100 basis points equal 1 percentage point.

SEBI price‑band rule lifts foreign ETF prices above NAV

Investors face premium risk as SEBI’s new price‑band lifts ETF prices

  • SEBI introduced a price‑band rule for foreign ETFs.
  • Rule pushed ETF market prices above their NAVs.
  • Investors risk losses if the artificial premium collapses.
ETF distribution / mutual fund industry

What happened

On 13 September 2026, SEBI implemented a price‑band rule that caps the trading range of foreign‑listed exchange‑traded funds (ETFs) for Indian investors. Combined with existing foreign investment limits, the rule caused market prices of these ETFs to trade at a premium to their net asset values (NAVs), exposing buyers to potential losses if the premium narrows.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. SEBI’s price‑band rule and foreign investment caps raise allowed price range for foreign ETFs
  2. Traders price ETFs above their NAV, creating a market premium
  3. Investors purchase ETFs at inflated prices, increasing cost basis
  4. If the premium narrows, investors incur capital losses and fund inflows may decline

The regulatory rule expands the permissible trading band, prompting market participants to price foreign ETFs above their intrinsic NAV. This creates a premium that investors must pay, raising their effective cost. When the premium corrects, the higher cost translates into capital losses and may dampen future inflows, affecting earnings of distributors. The lag is short for price adjustments (days) but earnings impact appears over months.

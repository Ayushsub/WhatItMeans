RBI schedules Rs 25,000 crore bond sale auction for Oct 13

Six government paper tenors absorb bank cash balances on October 13.

  • RBI announced a Rs 25,000 crore government bond sale for October 13.
  • Selling bonds drains cash from the banking system, tightening liquidity conditions.
  • Auction yield outcomes and bank liquidity deficit levels post-auction.
Banking & Financials

What happened

The Reserve Bank of India announced plans to conduct an Open Market Operation sale of government securities worth Rs 25,000 crore on October 13. The auction will feature six distinct sovereign paper maturities ranging between 2030 and 2034. RBI will determine the accepted bid levels and allotment amounts following the auction process.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. step 1: RBI auctions Rs 25,000 crore of sovereign bonds to banks
  2. step 2: Banks transfer cash balances to RBI to pay for securities
  3. step 3: Systemic cash liquidity decreases by the auction value
  4. step 4: Short-term money market rates face upward pressure

When the RBI sells government securities in an open market operation, purchasing banks pay for these bonds using cash reserves held with the central bank. This transaction directly removes Rs 25,000 crore of rupee liquidity from the banking system within days of settlement. With less excess cash available, interbank borrowing rates often face upward pressure over subsequent weeks. Lenders may eventually pass on higher short-term funding costs to broader debt market instruments over a multi-quarter horizon.

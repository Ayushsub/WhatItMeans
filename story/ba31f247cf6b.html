0.4% merchant discount rate on UPI payments above ₹2,000 from Oct 15

Around 4% of merchant UPI transactions will now incur a 0.4% fee

  • UPI fees introduced for payments over ₹2,000
  • Large merchants and some consumers will face new charges
  • Watch fee implementation and merchant response
Banking & Financial ServicesRetail & E‑commerceTelecomInterest RatesCurrency

What happened

On 15 October 2026 the Finance Ministry notified that person‑to‑merchant (P2M) UPI transactions above ₹2,000 will attract a 0.4% merchant discount rate (MDR) with a maximum charge of ₹300 per transaction. Payments up to ₹2,000, person‑to‑person transfers and small‑merchant payments remain free, while capital‑market transactions will be charged 0.02% capped at ₹300. The fee split is expected to give banks about 40% of the proceeds.

large merchants (payments >₹2,000)

remain fee‑free, no direct impact

pay a 0.02% fee capped at ₹300, negligible for occasional investors
banks and NPCI
receive a new revenue stream from the MDR

Which sectors

  • Banking & Financial Services tailwind — new fee creates additional revenue share for banks and payment processors
  • Retail & E‑commerce mixed — large merchants face higher costs, but may pass on to consumers
  • Telecom headwind — flat ₹5 fee on telecom‑related UPI payments adds cost for providers

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: finance ministry notification imposes 0.4% MDR on UPI P2M payments above ₹2,000
  2. step 2: banks and NPCI are forced to collect the fee and split revenue, with banks getting ~40%
  3. step 3: merchants absorb the cost and may raise prices or limit acceptance, shifting some burden to consumers
  4. step 4: higher transaction cost appears in merchant margins and consumer bills, while banks record fee income

The policy change (step 1) obliges the payment ecosystem to levy a fee (step 2). Because the fee is collected at the point of sale, merchants see a direct cost increase (step 3). Over the next few weeks to months this cost can be reflected either as higher prices for goods/services or as reduced merchant profitability, while banks record the fee as non‑interest income (step 4).

India's forex reserves fall $4.9bn to $780.7bn as rupee steadies

RBI sold dollars to lift rupee to 95.87 amid falling reserves

  • Forex reserves dropped $4.9bn to $780.7bn.
  • Rupee rose to 95.87 after RBI intervention.
  • Watch RBI's capacity to support the currency.
Banking & FinancialsExportersCurrency

What happened

India's foreign exchange reserves fell by $4.9 billion to $780.7 billion, driven by lower gold valuations and a decline in foreign currency assets. On the same day the rupee halted its slide, closing at 95.87 per US dollar after the RBI sold dollars in the market and oil prices retreated. A later report noted the rupee steadied around 95.96.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. step 1: RBI sells dollars to support the rupee
  2. step 2: increased dollar supply pushes the rupee higher
  3. step 3: a stronger rupee lowers the rupee cost of imports and foreign debt service
  4. step 4: lower import input costs appear in corporate earnings and can ease inflation

The RBI's dollar sales raise the rupee’s value, which immediately reduces the local‑currency price of imported goods and foreign‑currency loans. Companies feel the impact with a lag of a few weeks as lower input costs flow through to earnings, while consumers may see modest price relief. The effect on inflation can feed back into monetary‑policy considerations over months.

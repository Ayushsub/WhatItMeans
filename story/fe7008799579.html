OPEC cuts India's 2026 oil demand growth to 40,000 bpd, sees 2027 surge

India’s oil demand growth slowed to just 40,000 barrels daily

  • OPEC lowered India's 2026 demand growth forecast
  • Higher global demand expected in 2027, led by India
  • Impacts fuel prices and related industries
petroleum refiningautomotiveagricultureCrude & Energy

What happened

On 17 October 2026 OPEC reduced its forecast for India’s oil demand growth in 2026 to 40,000 barrels per day, citing strong vehicle sales, manufacturing and agricultural activity. In the same release OPEC projected global oil demand to average 108.2 million barrels per day in 2027, up from 105.8 mb/d in 2026, with India and non‑OECD Asia driving most of the increase.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: OPEC revises India’s 2026 demand growth down to 40,000 bpd and lifts 2027 global demand outlook
  2. step 2: expectations of larger oil imports for India in 2027 and a slower growth path in 2026
  3. step 3: higher import bills translate into higher domestic fuel prices, raising costs for transport, agriculture and industry
  4. step 4: cost pressures appear in corporate earnings and consumer spending, while refiners may see higher throughput

The forecast change first alters market expectations about how much crude India will need next year. Importers then adjust procurement plans, which pushes up domestic fuel prices as global oil prices remain high. Higher fuel costs affect transport‑intensive businesses and household budgets, while refiners may process more crude if demand picks up in 2027, improving their volumes but not necessarily margins if price spreads stay tight. The lag from forecast to price impact is typically a few weeks, with earnings effects showing up in the next quarterly reports.

Indian shares rebound in early trade on softer crude oil and IT gains

Softer crude eases imported cost pressures across 30 benchmark Sensex companies.

  • Lower oil bills reduce import costs and help domestic inflation pressures.

What happened

On October 17, 2026, Indian equity benchmarks opened higher and rebounded in morning trade, buoyed by declining crude oil prices. Gains in heavyweights including HDFC Bank, Tata Consultancy Services, and Infosys supported the recovery. Some prominent constituents, such as Maruti Suzuki, Power Grid, and Kotak Mahindra Bank, bucked the trend to trade lower.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over days

  1. Global crude oil prices retreat
  2. India's foreign currency import bill moderates
  3. Input and fuel cost pressures ease for domestic corporations
  4. Equity investor sentiment stabilizes, lifting benchmark large caps

A decline in international crude oil prices instantly lowers the expected foreign exchange outlay for India, which imports over 80 percent of its oil needs. Over days to weeks, this reduction works through supply chains to lower diesel and logistics expenses for manufacturers. Calmer inflation expectations then reduce macroeconomic pressure on domestic interest rates. With macroeconomic headwinds temporarily easing, institutional buyers rotate capital back into liquid market leaders such as major banks and IT exporters.

Market reaction

Bharat Electronics, Mahindra and Mahindra, Bajaj Finance, Trent, Tata Steel, TCS, Tech Mahindra, Infosys, and HDFC Bank advanced, while Maruti, Power Grid, and Kotak Mahindra Bank traded lower.

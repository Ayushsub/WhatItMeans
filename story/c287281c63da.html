Draft note proposes rupee payments for SEZ services to domestic buyers

SEZ firms could receive payments in rupees instead of dollars

  • Draft note suggests rupee settlement for SEZ services
  • Could reduce foreign‑exchange outflow and ease pricing for domestic buyers
  • Watch implementation details and market response

What happened

On 18 September 2026 the Economic Times reported a draft government note that would allow payments for services supplied by Special Economic Zones (SEZs) to domestic tariff area (DTA) entities to be made in rupees. Currently, Section 2(z) of the SEZ Act requires such service proceeds to be realised in foreign exchange, while goods supplies are exempt. The draft seeks to align the treatment of services with that of goods.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: draft note permits rupee settlement for SEZ services
  2. step 2: firms shift from foreign‑exchange invoicing to rupee invoicing
  3. step 3: domestic buyers face lower conversion fees and firms avoid FX volatility
  4. step 4: earnings reflect reduced currency costs and potentially higher order volumes

The policy change first removes the regulatory requirement to realise service proceeds in foreign exchange. That forces SEZ service providers to invoice in rupees, which in turn lowers the need for companies to buy dollars or euros for payments. The reduced transaction cost and currency risk can improve profit margins and make SEZ services cheaper for Indian buyers, showing up as higher revenue or lower expense lines in quarterly results. The lag is typically a few weeks for firms to adjust invoicing systems, with earnings impact appearing in the next quarter.

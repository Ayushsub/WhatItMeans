India faces export tariff risk as US trade talks stall, RBI chief says

RBI chief warns US deal delay could hit exporters

  • US‑India trade deal stalls
  • Potential tariffs could pressure Indian exporters
  • Watch other FTAs and US tariff moves

What happened

On 16 October 2026 the RBI chief said the failure to finalise a trade deal with the United States may hurt India by exposing it to a tariff shock. He added that diversification of export markets and products, together with new free‑trade agreements (FTAs), can cushion the impact. The comment linked the trade‑policy uncertainty to possible higher US duties on Indian goods.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: US‑India trade negotiations stall
  2. step 2: US may impose higher tariffs on Indian exports
  3. step 3: Indian exporters face reduced demand or higher costs
  4. step 4: earnings pressure for export‑dependent firms and possible price rise for consumers

The stalled talks trigger a policy risk that forces the US to consider tariff measures. Those tariffs raise the landed cost of Indian goods, which forces exporters to either cut margins or lose orders. The reduced margins or lost sales flow through to company earnings, while higher import prices can feed through to consumer price indices. The lag is typically a few weeks for tariff announcements, then months for trade‑flow adjustments to show up in earnings.

Optimists argue that existing FTAs with the EU, ASEAN and other partners will absorb the US market shortfall, limiting revenue loss.

Pessimists contend that the US is a core market for many Indian exporters and alternative FTAs cannot fully replace the lost demand, leading to noticeable earnings hits.

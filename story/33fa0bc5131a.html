US waives threatened 100% tariffs on Indian imports of Russian crude

Discounted Russian crude flows continue after Washington drops 100% punitive import penalties.

  • US waived secondary tariff penalties of up to 100% on Russian oil buyers.
  • Indian refiners retain access to cheap crude, protecting domestic fuel costs and margins.
  • Trade talks and potential reinstatement of tariff threats by Washington remain key risks.
Oil and gasExport-oriented manufacturingTrade & Tariffs

What happened

On October 17, 2026, US President Donald Trump issued a waiver on the Graham law provisions that threatened tariffs of up to 100% on nations importing Russian crude, including India. The measure followed earlier threats proposing tariffs as high as 500% before tapering to 100% and then being suspended. The reprieve permits India, which recently surpassed China as the leading buyer of Russian crude, to continue purchases without immediate trade penalties.

Who is affected

Indian oil refiners
Avoid export disruption and maintain access to discounted feedstock without immediate US sanctions.
Indian goods exporters to the US
Escape severe margin compression that would have resulted from threatened 100% US border duties.
Domestic fuel consumers
Face lower risk of near-term fuel price spikes as refiners preserve cheaper crude sourcing channels.

Which sectors

  • Oil and gas tailwind — Refiners sustain lower raw material costs without facing immediate penalties from Washington.
  • Export-oriented manufacturing tailwind — Immediate risk of retaliatory tariffs on US-bound merchandise trade has been averted.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. US administration issues waiver on Graham law sanction provisions
  2. Indian refiners continue loading discounted Russian crude without border duty penalties
  3. Input costs for petroleum products stay contained across the domestic supply chain
  4. Refining margins stabilize and outbound manufacturing exports avoid sudden tariff shocks

The waiver halts the enforcement of punitive US border duties within days, removing immediate export anxiety for Indian businesses. Over weeks, Indian refiners can contract seaborne Russian oil supplies without the operational threat of US secondary sanctions. Sourcing discounted crude keeps refinery input costs lower over several months, moderating domestic inflation pressures. However, because the waiver can be revoked unilaterally, an underlying risk premium remains embedded in planning cycles.

Jargon, in plain words

Waiver

Secondary sanctions

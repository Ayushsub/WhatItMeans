Rupee reverses september gains as crude oil prices rise, RBI says

What happened

On September 26, 2026, the RBI bulletin noted that the rupee, which had earlier risen on inflows from FCNR(B) deposits, fell after crude oil prices increased. The central bank’s director general highlighted the reversal in September.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. step 1: crude oil prices rise
  2. step 2: larger import bill pressures foreign‑exchange reserves and pushes rupee down
  3. step 3: weaker rupee raises import‑linked costs for companies and consumers
  4. step 4: depreciation appears in exchange‑rate movements and inflation data

Higher crude prices increase the dollar outflow needed for oil imports, which squeezes the RBI's foreign‑exchange reserves and creates downward pressure on the rupee. The currency weakness then raises the rupee cost of imported inputs, feeding into corporate margins and consumer price indices. The effect typically shows up in exchange‑rate quotes within days and in inflation numbers after a few weeks.

What happened

In the week ending 22 August 2026, gold climbed roughly 5% to around $4,603 per ounce, a three‑month high. The rally represents a 17% gain since the June lows and is linked to a weaker U.S. dollar and rising Indian festive‑season buying.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. step 1: a weaker U.S. dollar lowers the dollar price of gold
  2. step 2: the lower dollar price translates into a cheaper rupee price for Indian buyers
  3. step 3: cheaper gold spurs jewellery purchases, imports and ETF inflows ahead of festivals
  4. step 4: higher sales and loan demand boost earnings for jewellers and banks
  5. step 5: earnings improvements appear in quarterly results and loan‑book growth

The dollar’s weakness first reduces the quoted price of gold in dollars. Because Indian buyers pay in rupees, the effective cost falls, prompting more jewellery purchases and import orders as the August‑September wedding season begins. Retailers stock more, and investors add to gold ETFs, which together lift revenues for jewellery makers and increase loan demand for banks that finance gold purchases. These effects typically show up in earnings over the next few quarters.

Market reaction

RBI likely to hike repo rate by up to 75 bps, pushing bond yields higher

Expect a 50‑75 basis-point repo hike as food and oil prices surge

  • RBI may raise repo rate by 50‑75 bps
  • Higher rates could lift bond yields and borrowing costs
  • Watch inflation data and RBI’s policy decision
fixed incomeInterest RatesInflation

What happened

Experts expect the Reserve Bank of India to increase the repo rate by 50‑75 basis points. The move is driven by rising food and oil prices that heighten inflation risks. Consequently, bond yields are projected to peak in October.

Who is affected

short‑term corporate bond investors
potentially better risk‑reward as yields rise, modest upside
borrowers with floating‑rate loans
higher interest payments, cost increase roughly matching the rate hike
dynamic bond fund investors

Which sectors

  • fixed income mixed — rate rise lifts new issue yields but depresses existing bond prices

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: RBI hikes the repo rate by 50‑75 bps
  2. step 2: short‑term borrowing costs rise, pushing up government and corporate bond yields
  3. step 3: higher yields increase interest expenses for corporates and raise financing costs for consumers
  4. step 4: earnings pressure appears in corporate profit reports and bond fund valuations adjust

The RBI’s policy change raises the cost of short‑term funds, which forces banks to price loans higher. This cost pass‑through lifts yields on both sovereign and corporate bonds. Companies with debt see larger interest expenses, while consumers with floating‑rate loans face higher repayments. The impact shows up later in quarterly earnings and in the net asset values of bond‑focused funds, typically over a few months.

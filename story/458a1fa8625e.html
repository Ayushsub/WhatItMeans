RBI forex reserves drop $60 billion in six weeks to defend rupee

India's foreign exchange stockpile shrank by $8.58 billion in a single week.

  • Forex reserves fell $60 billion in six weeks to $726 billion on October 9.
  • Central bank dollar sales absorb local currency liquidity and reduce import cover buffers.
  • Weekly RBI statistical releases showing further movements in foreign exchange reserves.
BankingOil & GasCurrency

What happened

India's foreign exchange reserves fell to $726 billion as of October 9, down from $785.7 billion on September 4. This represents a depletion of $59.7 billion over six weeks as the Reserve Bank of India sold dollars to defend the rupee. In the week ending October 9 alone, reserves dropped by $8.58 billion.

Who is affected

import-dependent companies
Central bank dollar sales stabilize the exchange rate, reducing sudden raw material cost increases.
short-term debt borrowers
Withdrawal of rupee liquidity through dollar sales can nudge domestic short-term borrowing costs higher.

Which sectors

  • Banking mixed — Dollar intervention absorbs rupees from the banking system, tightening systemic liquidity while curbing currency volatility.
  • Oil & Gas tailwind — Intervention prevents sharp rupee depreciation, keeping landed costs for imported crude oil more predictable.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. RBI sells US dollars in the foreign exchange market
  2. Commercial banks pay rupees to RBI to receive dollars
  3. Rupee liquidity in the domestic banking system contracts
  4. Short-term money market rates face upward pressure while foreign reserves decline

To prevent sharp rupee depreciation, the RBI sells dollars directly into the forex market. When commercial banks buy these dollars, they surrender rupees to the central bank, which drains cash out of the domestic banking system within days. Over subsequent weeks, tighter rupee liquidity can elevate overnight borrowing rates between banks. Over months, repeated intervention reduces overall foreign currency reserves available for future macroeconomic shocks.

Depends on:

RBI rate hike unlikely to cause widespread NBFC asset quality stress

On Oct 3, analysts say a rate hike may spare NBFCs from major stress

  • Nuvama report says RBI hike unlikely to hurt NBFC asset quality
  • Historical trends show rate hikes rarely trigger broad loan defaults
  • Watch RBI policy moves and NBFC credit metrics
Banking & FinancialsInterest RatesCredit & Liquidity

What happened

On 3 October 2026, Nuvama Institutional Equities released a report stating that a potential RBI policy rate increase is unlikely to cause broad‑based stress to the asset quality of non‑banking financial companies (NBFCs). The report cites past episodes where rate hikes alone did not lead to widespread loan deterioration. It also notes that current external factors, such as the West Asia situation, are being monitored but are not expected to change the conclusion.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. step 1: RBI raises the policy repo rate
  2. step 2: borrowing costs for NBFCs increase
  3. step 3: loan borrowers face higher repayment burdens
  4. step 4: potential pressure on NBFC loan portfolios
  5. step 5: historically, this pressure has not translated into large‑scale asset quality deterioration

When the RBI lifts its repo rate, NBFCs typically face higher funding costs, which they may pass on to borrowers through higher loan rates. Higher borrower costs can strain repayment capacity, creating a risk of loan defaults. However, past cycles have shown that NBFCs' diversified funding sources and credit underwriting standards have limited the translation of this stress into widespread non‑performing assets, so earnings and credit metrics tend to remain stable. The lag from rate change to any observable impact on NBFC earnings is usually a few weeks to a couple of months.

Jargon, in plain words

NBFC
non‑banking financial company, a firm that offers loans and other financial services but does not hold a banking licence
asset quality
the health of a lender's loan book, measured by the proportion of loans that are performing versus those that are in default
policy repo rate
the interest rate at which the RBI lends short‑term funds to banks, influencing overall market rates
non‑performing assets (NPA)
loans where borrowers are not making interest or principal payments as scheduled

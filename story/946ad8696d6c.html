Rupee steadies near 95.8 as banks sell dollars, range 94.5‑96 expected

Public sector banks sold dollars, pushing rupee to 95.81 per dollar

  • Rupee rose to 95.81 per dollar
  • Intervention curbs further appreciation
  • Watch RBI actions and dollar inflows
Banking & FinancialsImport‑heavy industriesCurrencyCredit & Liquidity

What happened

The Indian rupee appreciated by 15 paise to close at 95.81 against the US dollar on Friday, aided by large public‑sector banks selling dollars on RBI direction. Analysts expect the rupee to trade in a narrow 94.5‑96 band in the near term because fresh dollar inflows via FCNR deposits and external borrowings are not translating into a stronger currency.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. step 1: RBI directs large public‑sector banks to sell US dollars
  2. step 2: increased dollar supply eases upward pressure on the rupee
  3. step 3: a stronger rupee reduces the rupee cost of imports and foreign‑currency debt servicing
  4. step 4: import‑heavy companies see lower input costs while exporters see marginal revenue compression

The central bank’s order forces banks to dump dollars, which adds supply to the forex market and pushes the rupee up. The currency move happens within days, but the effect on corporate earnings unfolds over weeks as import contracts are settled and debt service schedules adjust. Exporters feel the impact later, as pricing cycles incorporate the steadier exchange rate.

Market reaction

The rupee rose 15 paise to 95.81 per dollar and benchmark bond yields edged higher, as reported by the Economic Times.

What optimists are arguing

Optimists argue that continued RBI‑led dollar sales and muted foreign inflows will keep the rupee confined to the 94.5‑96 band, supporting import‑cost stability and containing inflation.

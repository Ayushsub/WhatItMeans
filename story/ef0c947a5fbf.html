Trump signs sanctions law opening door to 100% tariffs on India

Indian exporters face the threat of 100% tariffs on US shipments.

  • US President Trump signed a law enabling 100% tariffs on Russian oil buyers.
  • India faces potential trade penalties but the White House retains flexible waiver power.
  • What to watch is whether the US enforces these tariffs or uses them as leverage.
Oil and Gas (Refining)Export-oriented sectorsTrade & Tariffs

What happened

On September 18, 2026, US President Donald Trump signed the Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 into law. This legislation targets Russia's energy and defense sectors, including its shadow fleet of tankers. Crucially, it grants the US administration the authority to impose tariffs of up to 100% on countries, including India and China, that continue to purchase Russian oil and gas.

Who is affected

Indian oil refiners
They face increased pressure to reduce imports of discounted Russian crude or risk triggering US trade penalties.
Indian exporters to the US
They face the potential threat of up to 100% tariffs on their shipments if bilateral trade tensions escalate under this law.

Which sectors

  • Oil and Gas (Refining) headwind — Refiners relying on cheap Russian crude may have to source more expensive alternatives to avoid US sanctions.
  • Export-oriented sectors mixed — While the law allows for 100% tariffs, the application is not automatic, leaving these sectors vulnerable to geopolitical negotiations.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. US enacts law allowing up to 100% tariffs on buyers of Russian energy
  2. Indian refiners must choose between cheap Russian crude and US market access
  3. Shifting away from Russian oil increases raw material costs for Indian refiners
  4. Higher input costs compress margins or force refiners to pass costs to consumers

The trigger is the signing of the Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, which immediately establishes the legal framework for tariffs. Over the coming weeks and months, Indian refiners will have to evaluate the risk of continuing Russian oil purchases against the threat of US trade retaliation. If refiners decide to diversify away from Russian crude, they will likely have to purchase more expensive oil from alternative global sources, a transition that typically takes quarters to reflect in supply contracts. Ultimately, these higher procurement costs will show up as compressed operating margins for oil marketing companies or higher fuel prices for Indian consumers.

The bull case

What optimists are arguing

Bulls argue that the law's flexible design means the US will use it primarily as negotiating leverage rather than actually imposing destructive 100% tariffs on a strategic partner like India.

Depends on:

  • US granting waivers or exemptions to India
  • India successfully negotiating bilateral trade terms with the US

The bear case

What pessimists are arguing

Bears argue that the law creates persistent policy uncertainty that could deter investment in Indian export sectors and force refiners to abandon cheap Russian crude, raising India's energy import bill.

Depends on:

  • Strict enforcement of the sanctions by the Trump administration
  • A lack of viable, cheap alternative crude sources for Indian refiners

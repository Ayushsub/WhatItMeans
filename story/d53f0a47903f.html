RBI to sell $2.6 bn bonds and tighten cash reserve ratio

Banks face a higher cash reserve ratio as RBI sells $2.6 bn bonds

  • RBI announced $2.6 bn bond sale and stricter CRR from Oct 16.
  • Tighter reserves reduce bank liquidity, affecting credit supply.
  • Impact on loan growth and bond yields.
Banking & FinancialsCredit & Liquidity

What happened

The Reserve Bank of India said it will sell about $2.6 billion of government bonds. From 16 October, banks will have to meet a stricter cash reserve ratio (CRR) requirement. The move is intended to tighten liquidity in the banking system.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. step 1: RBI announces $2.6 bn bond sale and a stricter CRR
  2. step 2: banks must allocate a larger share of deposits to cash reserves
  3. step 3: excess liquidity for lending and market‑making shrinks
  4. step 4: loan growth slows and short‑term rates face upward pressure
  5. step 5: banks see lower net interest margins and bond yields rise

The policy announcement forces banks to keep more cash on the RBI’s books, which immediately drains surplus liquidity. Because banks have less free funds, they cut back on new loan disbursements, a lag that typically shows up within a few weeks. The reduced lending pressure and higher demand for safe assets push short‑term rates up and raise government bond yields, which then appear in banks' earnings as narrower interest margins. The full effect on earnings and bond markets usually unfolds over weeks to a few months.

Trump pressures Fed chair Warsh to cut rates as markets price a hike

Trump touts 15% growth if rates are cut, sparking Fed clash

  • Markets still expect a hike

What happened

On September 5, 2026 President Donald Trump publicly asked Federal Reserve chair Kevin Warsh to lower the federal funds rate, claiming a cut could unleash 15% economic growth. At the same time, market participants were pricing in a rate hike at the Fed’s upcoming meeting, citing a strong jobs report and pending inflation data. The clash created uncertainty over the Fed’s next policy move.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. step 1: Trump publicly pushes the Fed to cut rates
  2. step 2: The Fed faces political pressure, influencing its policy outlook
  3. step 3: Rate expectations move the US dollar either weaker (cut) or stronger (hike)
  4. step 4: Dollar movements affect the rupee‑dollar exchange rate
  5. step 5: Exchange‑rate shifts alter export competitiveness and import costs, showing up in corporate earnings

The president’s demand creates a political signal that the Fed must consider, which can shift market expectations for the federal funds rate. Those expectations translate into dollar‑rupee movements within days to weeks. A weaker dollar benefits Indian exporters and IT firms, while a stronger dollar raises the rupee’s value, hurting import‑dependent sectors and increasing the cost of dollar‑denominated debt. The earnings impact appears in quarterly results as margin changes. The lag from policy talk to earnings is typically weeks to a few months.

Market reaction

Markets were pricing in a rate hike despite Trump’s push, according to the Mint and CNBC reports.

The bull case

What optimists are arguing

Optimists argue that a Fed cut would lower global financing costs, spur US consumption and lift demand for Indian exports, supporting earnings growth.

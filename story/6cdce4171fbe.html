Nifty slides below 24,000 for fourth consecutive week

Four weeks of losses coincide with 7.8% GDP growth

  • Nifty fell below 24,000 for a fourth week
  • Market weakness contrasts with strong GDP growth
  • Watch support‑resistance levels and oil price trends

What happened

On 5 September 2026 the Nifty 50 closed below the 24,000 mark, marking its fourth straight weekly decline. The index posted an 8.60% loss year‑to‑date despite India reporting 7.80% GDP growth in Q1 FY27. Technical analysis highlighted support around 23,750‑23,700 and resistance near 24,150‑24,200.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. step 1: soaring crude oil prices and weak leading stocks depress market sentiment
  2. step 2: lower sentiment forces investors to cut equity exposure
  3. step 3: reduced demand pushes the Nifty lower and keeps Bank Nifty range‑bound
  4. step 4: lower index levels translate into weaker fund inflows and pressure on bank loan growth

Higher oil prices raise input costs for many companies, which together with a slowdown in top‑line earnings of leading stocks squeezes profit expectations. Investors react by selling equities, driving the Nifty down. The fall in the index curtails fresh inflows into equity‑linked mutual funds, limiting banks' ability to raise capital through market channels and dampening loan‑growth prospects. The lag from oil price moves to earnings impact is a few weeks, while the translation into fund flows occurs over the next month.

Market reaction

The sources reported that markets extended their losses, with the Nifty slipping below 24,000 and the Bank Nifty remaining range‑bound amid bearish pressure.

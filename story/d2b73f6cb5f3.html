SEBI notifies 2026 settlement rules and plans closing auction norms

Securities market violators can resolve cases faster through a ₹10 lakh fast-track route.

  • SEBI notified Settlement Regulations 2026 and announced upcoming guidelines on closing auction sessions.
  • The new formula prevents double counting of gains and offers fast-track dispute resolutions.
  • Detailed rules regarding derivative expiry settlements are expected within a week.
Capital Markets

What happened

On October 10, 2026, SEBI notified the Settlement Regulations 2026, revising how settlement amounts for securities violations are calculated. The framework separates disgorgement from settlement fees and introduces a ₹10 lakh fast-track route for eligible cases. SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey also confirmed that guidelines for closing auction sessions in derivatives will be issued within a week.

Who is affected

Entities accused of securities violations
They gain access to clearer fee calculations and a ₹10 lakh fast-track route to resolve regulatory disputes faster.
Derivatives traders
They face upcoming operational changes in how closing prices and settlements on expiry days are determined.

SEBI's revised formula eliminates the double counting of unjust gains and isolates disgorgement from routine penalty calculations. Alleged violators can opt for fast-track settlement pathways, such as the ₹10 lakh option, to resolve cases quickly without prolonged litigation. In parallel, new closing auction session rules will change how derivative contracts settle on expiry days. Over coming months, these measures aim to reduce legal overhead and clarify trading rules across the financial market.

Jargon, in plain words

Disgorgement
The forced repayment of illegally acquired profits back to a regulator or affected investors.
Closing Auction Session
A specialized trading window at the end of the market day designed to determine fair closing prices.
Settlement
A process allowing an entity under regulatory investigation to resolve charges by paying a fee without admitting guilt.

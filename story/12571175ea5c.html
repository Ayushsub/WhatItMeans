India must stay open to trade as domestic output may be costlier

Domestic production could cost more than imports, says principal secretary

  • India’s principal secretary warned domestic output may be pricier than imports
  • Staying open to global markets is urged to preserve economic resilience

What happened

On Oct 3, 2026, Principal Secretary P.K. Mishra told the Prime Minister that economic resilience should not mean insulating India from global markets. He highlighted that producing goods domestically could be more expensive than importing them, underscoring a need for better risk assessment. The comment came amid ongoing trade‑related actions over the previous week.

The comment acts as a trigger that forces policymakers to evaluate the trade‑off between self‑reliance and cost efficiency. That evaluation pushes companies to re‑examine their supply chains, which may shift some import volumes back home. The shift raises input costs, which then flow through to earnings margins for manufacturers and to retail prices for consumers. The lag between the statement and observable price or earnings impact typically spans a few months as firms adjust contracts and inventories.

Coal ministry confirms NTPC and TFL applied for Rs 37,500 crore gasification scheme

Fifty thousand industrial jobs and domestic urea output hinge on twenty-five planned conversion plants.

  • Coal ministry confirms NTPC and TFL bid for Rs 37,500 crore gasification incentives.
  • The scheme aims to drive 25 cleaner fuel projects and 50,000 new jobs.
  • Additional applications before September 7 deadline will determine broader industry uptake.
Energy & UtilitiesFertilizers

What happened

The Ministry of Coal clarified on September 5, 2026, that Talcher Fertilisers Limited (TFL) and NTPC Limited have applied under the government's Rs 37,500 crore coal gasification scheme, rebutting reports of zero interest. The government expects around 25 projects to be established under the program, generating roughly 50,000 direct and indirect jobs. The application window remains open until September 7, with TFL already constructing a coal-based ammonia-urea complex targeted for commissioning in FY 2027-28.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. Government opens financial aid under the Rs 37,500 crore gasification scheme
  2. Developers like TFL and NTPC submit bids to build gasification complexes
  3. Domestic synthesis gas replaces imported natural gas as feedstocks
  4. Multi-year capital expenditure increases asset bases while shifting input cost structures

State financial backing lowers the hurdle for deploying capital-intensive coal gasification technology. Because projects like TFL's urea facility take years to build, execution lags mean major capacity will only come online around FY 2027-28. Once active, replacing imported liquefied natural gas with domestic coal derivatives insulates industrial producers from international price swings. This transmission will eventually appear as lower feedstock import expenses and altered asset profiles in company balance sheets.

coal gasification

commissioning

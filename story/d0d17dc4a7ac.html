SEBI permits digitally signed Power of Attorney to ease onboarding for FPIs

Foreign portfolio investors face lower documentation friction entering Indian capital markets.

  • SEBI allows digitally signed Power of Attorney documents for foreign portfolio investors.
  • Digital signatures eliminate physical paperwork delays and reduce operational friction for global funds.
  • Watch new FPI registration pace and custodian onboarding turnaround times.
Capital Markets & Financial Intermediaries

What happened

Markets regulator SEBI issued a circular permitting the acceptance of digitally signed Power of Attorney (PoA) documents for Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs). The move is designed to simplify and accelerate the onboarding process for overseas investors seeking registration and access to Indian markets. It eliminates the requirement for physical signatures on PoA paperwork.

Who is affected

Foreign portfolio investors (FPIs)
Face shorter registration turnaround times and reduced operational costs when opening accounts in India.
Designated depository participants and custodians
Can process overseas client mandates digitally, lowering administrative overhead.

Permitting digital signatures removes the need to courier hard-copy legal agreements across international borders. Custodians and designated depository participants can verify and execute legal authorizations in hours rather than weeks. This shortens the time required for a newly registered foreign fund to begin deploying capital. Over subsequent quarters, reduced administrative drag can marginally improve the ease of doing business for global institutional allocators.

Jargon, in plain words

Foreign Portfolio Investor (FPI)
An overseas entity or fund that invests in Indian securities like shares, bonds, or derivatives without taking controlling stakes.
Power of Attorney (PoA)
A formal legal document authorizing another person or entity (like a custodian or broker) to act on an investor's behalf.

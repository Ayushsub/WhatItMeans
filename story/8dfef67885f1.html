India keeps broken rice exports open, banks on steady paddy procurement

8.08 lakh tonnes of broken rice shipped in 2025 despite past bans

  • No new export curbs announced
  • Export continuity supports farmer income and global market share
  • Watch domestic rice stocks and global price trends
agriculturefood processingexport servicesTrade & Tariffs

What happened

India did not impose any fresh curbs on broken rice exports and continues to allow shipments. The country shipped about 8.08 lakh tonnes of broken rice in calendar year 2025. The earlier ban imposed on 9 September 2022 has been lifted, and no further restrictions were announced.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: government keeps export policy unchanged
  2. step 2: exporters can ship broken rice abroad
  3. step 3: foreign demand sustains shipment volumes, keeping farmer procurement steady
  4. step 4: earnings for farmers and exporters stay robust, while domestic supply may tighten

The policy decision (step 1) removes a regulatory barrier, allowing exporters to continue sales (step 2). As overseas buyers keep ordering, shipment volumes stay near 8 lakh tonnes (step 3). This translates into steady farmgate prices and export revenues (step 4). Any lag comes from the time needed to move grain from fields to ports, typically a few weeks.

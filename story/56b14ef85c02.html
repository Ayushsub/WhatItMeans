Government doubles weekly wheat auction to 1.99 lakh tonnes to curb prices

A doubled auction supply of 1.99 lakh tonnes faces market caps that restrict buyers.

  • Government doubled weekly wheat auction quantity to 1.99 lakh tonnes.
  • Higher market supply aims to lower rising domestic wheat prices.
  • Whether bidder caps are relaxed to allow processors to buy more volume.
FMCG and Packaged FoodsAgricultural CommoditiesInflation

What happened

On October 31, 2026, the Indian government doubled its weekly wheat auction allocation to 1.99 lakh tonnes to control rising grain prices. However, the maximum purchase limit per bidder was kept unchanged during these auctions. Market experts questioned whether this supply increase would successfully lower prices without expanding purchasing limits for individual processors.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  2. step 2: Physical grain availability in open market increases
  3. step 3: Unchanged per-bidder caps limit single-buyer purchase scale
  4. step 4: Wholesale flour price reductions remain muted or delayed

The government releases state-held grain reserves into spot markets to relieve supply deficits. An increase in total auction volume normally forces open-market wholesale prices downward by increasing competition among sellers. However, because each buyer remains capped on the quantity they can win, large millers must source remaining needs outside auctions, delaying retail price stabilization over a horizon of several weeks.

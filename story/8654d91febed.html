CBI books Subhash Chandra over alleged ₹1,322 crore loss to LICHFL

A state-backed housing finance company faces a ₹1,322 crore loss from defaulted loans.

  • CBI booked Subhash Chandra for allegedly inflating his net worth to secure LICHFL loans.
  • The alleged fraud led to defaults, causing a ₹1,322 crore loss to LICHFL.
  • Legal proceedings and potential asset recovery actions under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code.
Banking & FinancialsReal Estate & InfraInflation

What happened

On September 5, 2026, the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) registered a First Information Report (FIR) against Essel Group promoter Subhash Chandra and others. The agency alleges that Chandra misrepresented facts and inflated his net worth certificates to secure loan facilities worth ₹980 crore from LIC Housing Finance Limited (LICHFL). These loans subsequently defaulted, resulting in an estimated loss of ₹1,322 crore to the lender.

Shareholders of LIC Housing Finance Limited
They face potential downward pressure on share value due to the recognition of a ₹1,322 crore loss and increased provisioning requirements.
Creditors and promoters of Essel Group companies
They face heightened scrutiny and potential delays in debt resolution processes as insolvency proceedings undergo deeper forensic investigation.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. Borrower allegedly submits inflated net worth certificates to secure large credit facilities
  2. Lender approves and disburses ₹980 crore based on misrepresented financial strength
  3. The borrower defaults on repayment obligations as the underlying projects or assets fail to generate cash flow
  4. Lender classifies the accounts as non-performing assets, leading to a ₹1,322 crore loss and reduced earnings

When a borrower allegedly inflates their net worth, a lender misjudges the risk and disburses credit that exceeds the borrower's actual repayment capacity. Once the business cash flows fail, the borrower defaults, forcing the lender to write down the asset value. This process typically takes quarters to years to manifest as loans transition from standard to non-performing assets. Ultimately, the lender must provision for these losses, directly reducing its net profits and capital adequacy ratios.

Jargon, in plain words

net worth
The value of all the assets owned by a person or company, minus the total value of all their liabilities or debts.
Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code
The Indian law that outlines a time-bound process for resolving insolvency among individuals and corporations to maximize asset value.
provisioning
Setting aside a portion of earnings by a financial institution to cover potential future losses from bad loans.

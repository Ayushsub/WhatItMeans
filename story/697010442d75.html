The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. step 1: energy prices stay high, keeping inflation elevated
  2. step 2: investors demand higher real returns, pushing yields up
  3. step 3: higher yields translate into costlier borrowing for companies and consumers
  4. step 4: earnings pressure and price adjustments appear in corporate reports

The high energy cost first sustains inflation expectations. That forces investors to require higher yields on safe‑asset Treasuries. Those yields become the benchmark for corporate and consumer borrowing, so higher rates erode profit margins and may be passed on as price hikes. The lag is typically a few weeks as bond markets move first, followed by corporate financing cycles.

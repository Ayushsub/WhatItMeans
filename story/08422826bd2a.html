India to keep Russian crude in its energy mix, says foreign minister

Jaishankar stresses Russian crude remains vital despite mounting external pressures

  • India will continue importing Russian crude
  • Maintaining supply aims to stabilise domestic fuel markets
  • Watch for geopolitical and price developments
oil & gasCrude & Energy

What happened

On 3 Oct 2026, External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar told a policy panel that Russian crude oil will remain part of India’s energy mix. He highlighted the need to sustain robust relations with Moscow amid growing external pressures. No specific import volumes or price figures were disclosed.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: policy stance to keep Russian crude imports
  2. step 2: forces a steady flow of Russian crude despite sanctions risk
  3. step 3: refiners receive reliable feedstock, reducing need for costlier alternatives
  4. step 4: earnings reflect steadier refining margins and fuel prices stay less volatile

The minister’s statement signals a policy choice (step 1) that pushes import planners to maintain contracts with Russian exporters (step 2). This reduces the likelihood of sudden supply gaps, so refiners can keep operating at planned capacities (step 3). The effect shows up in quarterly earnings as more stable refining margins and in consumer price indices as muted fuel‑price inflation. The lag between the policy announcement and observable earnings impact is typically a few quarters.

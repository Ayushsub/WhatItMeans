NSE sets IPO price band at Rs 1,700-1,785 as unlisted market faces slowdown

Ten public sector undertakings stand to gain Rs 12,802 crore from the exchange listing.

  • NSE announced its IPO price band of Rs 1,700 to Rs 1,785 per share.
  • The listing could drain liquidity from India's unlisted shadow market and reward state-run lenders.
  • Watch the subscription levels and the post-listing performance of pre-IPO investors facing lock-ins.
Banking & FinancialsCapital Markets

What happened

The National Stock Exchange (NSE) has announced an initial public offering (IPO) with a price band of Rs 1,700 to Rs 1,785 per share, following its Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) submission to SEBI on June 17, 2026. The public issue, which comes after nearly ten years of regulatory and legal delays, includes a six-month lock-in period for pre-IPO investors. Ten public sector undertakings (PSUs), including State Bank of India, are set to pocket a combined profit of approximately Rs 12,802 crore by selling their stakes at the upper price band.

Who is affected

unlisted market investors
Investors who bought NSE shares in the unlisted market at prices above Rs 1,785 face immediate paper losses.
public sector banks and undertakings
Ten state-run entities, including State Bank of India, will realize up to Rs 12,802 crore in profits from selling their stakes.
unlisted market brokers
Intermediaries in the shadow market face a sharp drop in trading volumes as their largest asset transitions to the public exchange.

Which sectors

  • Banking & Financials tailwind — State-run banks holding NSE shares will see a significant boost to their non-interest income and capital reserves from the stake sale.
  • Capital Markets mixed — While the NSE itself transitions to a highly regulated public platform, the unlisted shadow market faces a severe loss of trading volume.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. NSE launches its official public IPO at a price band of Rs 1,700-1,785
  2. Trading volumes for NSE shares shift from the informal unlisted market to the regulated exchange
  3. The unlisted shadow market loses its most heavily traded asset, which previously accounted for half of its volume
  4. Liquidity in the broader unlisted market dries up, depressing valuations of other unlisted startups and firms

The announcement of the IPO price band immediately anchors the valuation of NSE shares, creating a benchmark that is lower than recent unlisted market trades. Within weeks of the listing, trading activity will migrate to the official exchange, draining the unlisted market of its primary volume driver. This liquidity drain is expected to gradually pressure the valuations of other unlisted companies over the coming quarters, as brokers and investors redirect capital to the public market. Pre-IPO investors will also face a six-month lag before they can liquidate their holdings due to regulatory lock-in periods.

Jargon, in plain words

unlisted market
An informal, over-the-counter market where shares of companies not listed on official stock exchanges are traded.
price band
The minimum and maximum share price range within which investors can bid during an initial public offering.
lock-in period
A predetermined timeframe during which certain shareholders are legally prohibited from selling their stock.

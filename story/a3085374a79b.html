What happened

On 26 September 2026, West Texas Intermediate (WTI) futures traded below $94 a barrel, reflecting roughly a 2% decline in global oil prices. The dip was linked to reports that diplomatic talks between the United States and Iran on reopening the Strait of Hormuz were making progress. The Economic Times also noted the US is contemplating a ban on diesel exports, adding a supply‑side nuance to the market.

The diplomatic progress first reduces the geopolitical premium that traders add to oil, which forces a price correction within days. The price correction then translates into lower import expenditures for Indian refiners, a lag of about one to two weeks as contracts settle. Those cost savings flow through to downstream fuel prices and manufacturing input costs, showing up in consumer price index data and corporate earnings in the following quarter.

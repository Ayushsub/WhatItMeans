India's crude imports hit record 5.4 mb/d; diesel exports rise to 12‑month high

700,000 barrels per day of diesel shipped abroad amid domestic shortage

  • Record crude imports of 5.4 million b/d in October
  • Diesel exports climb to 12‑month high
  • Potential impact on domestic fuel prices
energytransportationmanufacturingCrude & EnergyTrade & Tariffs

What happened

India imported an estimated 5.4 million barrels per day (mb/d) of crude oil in October, the highest on record, driven by West Asia supply recovery and Atlantic cargoes. At the same time, diesel shipments abroad rose to about 700,000 barrels per day (b/d), up from 620,000 b/d in September, as a domestic shortage prompted higher exports. Both trends were reported on 31 October 2026 by The Hindu BusinessLine.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. record crude imports and diesel shortage trigger higher demand for diesel abroad
  2. limited domestic supply forces exporters to ship more diesel
  3. greater export volumes raise foreign earnings for traders and keep refineries running at high rates
  4. higher export and import activity shows up in company earnings and potentially lifts domestic diesel prices

The surge in crude arrivals raises refinery runs, which in turn increases diesel output. A concurrent shortage in the domestic market pushes traders to divert more diesel for export, as seen in the 700,000 b/d figure. The extra export volume improves earnings for exporters and keeps refineries busy, while the tighter local supply can push retail diesel prices up. The lag from import to price impact is typically a few weeks as inventories adjust.

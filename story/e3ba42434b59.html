RBI releases data on forex inflows from special dollar-rupee swap window

Indian banks face an August 31 deadline for a special RBI dollar-swapping window.

  • RBI published data on foreign currency inflows under its special swap facility.
  • The facility helps Indian banks secure stable foreign currency deposits and manage rupee liquidity.
  • Total inflows gathered before the FCNR(B) swap window closes on August 31, 2026.
Banking & Financials

What happened

On August 22, 2026, the Reserve Bank of India released data on foreign exchange inflows coming through its special USD-INR swap facility. This facility, which was introduced on June 08, 2026, covers Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) deposits, External Commercial Borrowings, and Overseas Foreign Currency Borrowings. The special window for FCNR(B) deposits is scheduled to remain open until August 31, 2026.

Who is affected

Indian commercial banks
They gain access to a structured mechanism to swap foreign currency deposits into rupees, reducing their hedging costs until August 31, 2026.
Non-Resident Indians (NRIs)
They may receive more attractive interest rates on FCNR(B) accounts as domestic banks compete to attract foreign currency before the deadline.

Which sectors

  • Banking & Financials tailwind — The swap window allows banks to convert foreign currency deposits into rupees at favorable rates, lowering their overall funding costs and boosting liquidity.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. RBI offers a special USD-INR swap facility to commercial banks
  2. Banks aggressively solicit foreign currency deposits like FCNR(B) from non-residents
  3. Banks swap these dollars with the RBI for rupees at a predetermined rate
  4. Rupee liquidity in the domestic banking system increases, lowering short-term borrowing costs

When the RBI opens a swap window, it incentivizes banks to bring in foreign currency by offering a cheaper way to hedge exchange rate risk. For an investor with banking exposure, this triggers a push by banks to attract NRI deposits, which typically takes a few weeks to show up in deposit growth numbers. Once these funds are swapped with the RBI, the banking system receives a direct injection of rupee liquidity, easing domestic credit conditions. The final impact on bank net interest margins and lending rates usually materializes over one to two quarters.

The bull case

What optimists are arguing

Optimists argue that the swap facility successfully bolsters India's foreign exchange reserves and stabilizes the rupee by attracting steady, long-term NRI deposits.

Depends on:

  • strong participation from non-resident Indians
  • stable global interest rate differentials

The bear case

What pessimists are arguing

Pessimists point out that relying on temporary swap windows to boost liquidity can mask underlying structural deficits in domestic deposit growth once the window closes on August 31, 2026.

Depends on:

  • a sharp drop in foreign inflows post-August deadline
  • rising cost of foreign currency funding globally

Jargon, in plain words

FCNR(B) deposits
Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) deposits, which are bank accounts held in India by non-resident Indians denominated in foreign currencies like US dollars.
Forex Swap
A transaction where two parties exchange currencies for a certain period and agree to reverse the transaction at a later date.
External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs)
Loans secured by Indian companies from foreign lenders in foreign currencies.

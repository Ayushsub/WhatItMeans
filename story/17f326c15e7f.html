RBI to reassess growth and inflation after crude price shock

RBI’s Monetary Policy Committee faces a crude price shock

  • RBI governor says MPC will review outlook next month
  • Crude price surge could pressure inflation and growth
  • Watch RBI’s policy decision and inflation data
banking & financialsInterest RatesInflation

What happened

On 12 September 2026 RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra said the Monetary Policy Committee will reassess both growth and inflation dynamics at its meeting next month, citing a recent shock from crude oil prices. The comment signals that the central bank is closely monitoring the impact of higher oil prices on the economy.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: crude oil price shock lifts import bill and pushes headline inflation up
  2. step 2: higher inflation forces RBI to consider tightening monetary policy
  3. step 3: tighter policy raises policy rate, which lifts loan and bond yields for banks and borrowers
  4. step 4: higher yields appear as increased interest income for banks and higher financing costs for consumers

The oil price jump first raises the cost of imported fuel, feeding through to consumer prices and pushing inflation higher. Because the RBI targets price stability, a sustained rise pressures the Monetary Policy Committee to contemplate a policy‑rate increase. A higher policy rate translates into higher benchmark rates that banks use to price loans and deposits, so borrowers pay more while banks earn more on the interest side. The effect on earnings or household cash‑flows typically shows up with a lag of a few weeks to a couple of months as new loan pricing is implemented and existing contracts reprice.

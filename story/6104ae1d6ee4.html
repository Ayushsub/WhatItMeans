Bandhan Life executive expects diverse corporate earnings to attract foreign investors

Foreign investors sold Indian equities for two years over high valuations.

  • Foreign institutional investors remained net sellers in India over the past two years.
  • A return of foreign capital could support domestic equity valuations and market liquidity.
  • What to watch is whether corporate earnings diversify and accelerate to justify premium valuations.
Indian equitiesGrowth

What happened

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) have remained net sellers in the Indian equity market over the past two years. According to Avinash Agarwal of Bandhan Life, this selling pressure was driven by India's premium valuations and a slowdown in corporate earnings. However, he suggests that India's diverse earnings landscape could soon turn the tide and attract these investors back.

Who is affected

foreign institutional investors
They may reallocate capital back to Indian equities if corporate earnings growth diversifies and accelerates.
domestic equity investors
They could see increased liquidity and valuation support if foreign capital inflows resume.

Which sectors

  • Indian equities tailwind — A potential return of foreign institutional investors would increase buying pressure and support overall stock prices.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. Indian corporate earnings growth diversifies across multiple sectors
  2. Premium valuations of Indian stocks become justified by stronger financial performance
  3. Foreign institutional investors shift from net sellers to net buyers
  4. Increased capital inflows boost domestic stock market liquidity and prices

The process begins when corporate earnings across various sectors show robust and diverse growth, which typically takes a few quarters to reflect in financial statements. Once these earnings materialize, foreign investors evaluate the growth relative to India's premium valuations, a process that can take weeks of analysis. If the growth justifies the high prices, FIIs will begin reallocating capital, shifting their stance from net sellers to net buyers. This capital inflow gradually increases market liquidity and exerts upward pressure on stock prices over subsequent months.

Jargon, in plain words

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs)
Large investment funds, such as mutual funds or pension funds, based outside India that invest in Indian financial markets.
Premium valuations
A situation where stock prices are high relative to their underlying earnings or book value, indicating high market expectations.

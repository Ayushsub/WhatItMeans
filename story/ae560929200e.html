India trade deficit narrows to $26.86 billion as gold imports plunge

Indian engineering and textile exporters shipped goods worth $43.81 billion in August.

  • India's trade deficit narrowed to $26.86 billion in August as exports surged.
  • Faster export growth and lower gold imports ease pressure on the Indian rupee.
  • Whether global demand holds up to sustain this decade-high export growth pace.

What happened

In August 2026, India's merchandise trade deficit narrowed to $26.86 billion from $27.2 billion in the same month last year. Outbound shipments grew at a decade-high pace of 26.12% to reach $43.81 billion, driven by engineering, petroleum, and textile sectors. Meanwhile, overall imports rose 14.1% to $70.67 billion, capped by a 57.7% plunge in gold imports to $2.3 billion.

Gold importers and jewelers
A 57.7% drop in gold imports to $2.3 billion indicates sharply reduced local transaction volumes and inventory building.

A 26.12% surge in overall exports provides immediate revenue boosts and higher capacity utilization for these manufacturing segments.

Which sectors

  • Gems and Jewellery headwind — A 57.7% plunge in gold imports points to cooling domestic demand and higher raw material costs.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. Global demand rises for Indian engineering, petroleum, and textile products
  2. Domestic exporters increase shipments, raising total export value to $43.81 billion
  3. Concurrently, high prices deter domestic gold buyers, cutting gold imports by 57.7%
  4. The net outflow of foreign currency shrinks, narrowing the trade deficit to $26.86 billion

The process begins when international buyers increase orders for Indian manufactured goods, which translates into higher export revenues over a lag of one to two months as shipments clear. Simultaneously, elevated international gold prices discourage domestic buyers, leading to an immediate drop in import volumes. As these two forces interact, the gap between total imports and exports shrinks. This narrower trade deficit reduces the net demand for foreign currency to pay for imports, easing depreciation pressure on the Indian rupee within weeks.

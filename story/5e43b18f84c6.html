India's Bloomberg Global Aggregate Index entry delayed over market access

Potential $15‑25 billion inflow stalls as market access remains unresolved

  • India's BGAI inclusion delayed
  • Foreign inflows may be postponed
  • Watch bond index decision and yield spreads
financials (bond market)Institutional Flows

What happened

The Bloomberg Global Aggregate Bond Index (BGAI) has not added India yet, with the delay attributed to unresolved operational market access. Analysts estimate that inclusion could attract between $15 billion and $25 billion of foreign inflows. The hurdle remains a key factor before any index entry can occur.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: Bloomberg requires operational market access for index inclusion
  2. step 2: Delay means Indian bonds stay excluded from the index
  3. step 3: Exclusion prevents passive foreign fund allocations of $15‑25 bn
  4. step 4: Lower demand keeps sovereign yields higher
  5. step 5: Higher yields raise borrowing costs for government and corporates
  6. step 6: Costs appear as higher interest expense in earnings and higher coupon payments

The index’s rule forces a market‑access test; because the test is not met, Indian bonds remain out of the basket. This forces fund managers to allocate elsewhere, so the projected $15‑25 bn of passive inflows does not materialise. With demand muted, bond yields stay above what they would be with index inclusion, translating into higher coupon rates for new issuances. The effect on earnings and financing costs shows up with a lag of a few months as new debt is priced and serviced.

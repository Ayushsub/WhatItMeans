Indian bond yields jump as RBI plans ₹1 lakh crore sale to drain liquidity

Debt investors face sudden portfolio losses after a sharp 16 basis point yield spike.

  • RBI plans to sell ₹1 lakh crore of bonds to absorb excess banking liquidity.
  • Rising yields increase borrowing costs across the economy, hurting corporate and retail borrowers.
  • The upcoming October RBI policy meeting and global crude oil price movements.

What happened

On September 15, 2026, Indian government bond prices fell sharply, causing the benchmark 2031 bond yield to jump by 16 basis points. This sell-off was triggered by the Reserve Bank of India's plan to sell ₹1 lakh crore of bonds to drain excess liquidity from the banking system. Additionally, analysts at Citi projected potential interest rate hikes of 50 to 75 basis points starting in October 2026 due to rising oil and inflation risks.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. RBI announces ₹1 lakh crore bond sale to drain excess cash from banks
  2. Increased supply of bonds in the secondary market drives bond prices down
  3. Falling bond prices cause market yields to jump immediately
  4. Banks and corporate lenders raise their lending rates to match higher market yields

The transmission begins instantly in the financial markets as traders price in the massive ₹1 lakh crore bond supply, causing the 2031 yield to surge by 16 basis points. Over the next few weeks, this tighter liquidity forces banks to compete harder for deposits, raising their cost of funds. Consequently, within one to two quarters, these higher funding costs are passed on to retail and corporate borrowers in the form of pricier loans.

Jargon, in plain words

basis points
A financial unit of measurement equal to one-hundredth of a percentage point (0.01%), used to describe changes in interest rates or yields.

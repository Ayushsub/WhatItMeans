US Dollar slides and Yen surges as traders await key inflation data

Global currency markets face turbulence in September as the Japanese Yen surges.

  • The US Dollar fell while the Japanese Yen surged amid shifting interest rate expectations.
  • Fluctuations in global currencies alter import costs and foreign investment flows for India.
  • Upcoming US inflation data will influence the Federal Reserve's next interest rate decision.
Information TechnologyOil and GasInterest RatesInflation

What happened

During the week ending September 4, 2026, the US Dollar recorded a weekly loss as traders weighed mixed signals regarding the Federal Reserve's upcoming policy moves. Concurrently, a surging Japanese Yen caused ripples across global currency markets. Investors remained cautious and on edge ahead of a pivotal US inflation report.

Indian importers paying in US Dollars
A weaker US Dollar can lower the landing cost of imported raw materials, easing margin pressures.
Indian exporters with US Dollar revenues
A depreciating US Dollar relative to other currencies could reduce the rupee value of their overseas earnings.
Foreign portfolio investors in India
Shifting global interest rate expectations alter the relative attractiveness of Indian equities and bonds.

Which sectors

  • Information Technology headwind — Indian IT firms earn a significant portion of their revenue in US Dollars, meaning a weaker dollar can reduce their converted rupee earnings.
  • Oil and Gas tailwind — Crude oil is priced in US Dollars, so a softer dollar can lower the import bill for Indian refiners.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. Mixed economic signals shift expectations around US Federal Reserve interest rate cuts.
  2. Global investors adjust currency allocations, selling US Dollars and buying the Japanese Yen.
  3. A weaker US Dollar reduces the cost of dollar-denominated commodities like crude oil.
  4. Lower import bills ease input cost pressures for Indian manufacturing and refining companies.

When traders anticipate that the US Federal Reserve might cut interest rates, they often sell US Dollars in search of yields elsewhere, causing immediate currency market adjustments. This shift is amplified when other currencies, like the Japanese Yen, strengthen rapidly, altering global capital flows within days. For India, a weaker dollar typically translates to lower costs for dollar-priced imports like crude oil, though this relief takes weeks to pass through to corporate supply chains. Ultimately, these shifting currency dynamics show up in the quarterly earnings of import-dependent sectors as reduced raw material expenses.

The US Dollar slid to start September and posted a weekly loss, while the Japanese Yen surged, reverberating across global currency markets.

Jargon, in plain words

Carry trade
A financial strategy where investors borrow money in a currency with low interest rates (like the Yen) to invest in assets with higher returns elsewhere.
Inflation report
A periodic economic release that measures the rate at which the prices of goods and services are rising.

SEBI plans Demat 2.0 and tokenised bond trading

Tokenised corporate bonds could soon reach secondary markets under Demat 2.0.

  • SEBI plans Demat 2.0 alongside graded compliance for brokers.
  • Tokenised corporate bonds will become tradable in secondary markets.
  • Detailed regulatory frameworks and implementation timelines from SEBI.
capital marketsnon-banking financial companies

What happened

On October 24, 2026, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) announced plans to introduce Demat 2.0. Under the new architecture, tokenised corporate bonds will be made available for secondary-market trading. The regulator also proposed a graded compliance framework tailored for stockbrokers.

Who is affected

retail debt investors
Secondary-market access to tokenised corporate bonds could significantly lower transaction friction and minimum investment thresholds.
stockbrokers
A graded compliance system will scale regulatory requirements based on operational size and risk profile rather than applying a single uniform standard.

Which sectors

  • capital markets tailwind — Modernised depository infrastructure and tokenised bond trading could expand trading volumes and product offerings across brokerages.
  • non-banking financial companies tailwind — Deeper secondary liquidity for corporate bonds can facilitate easier market-based borrowing.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. SEBI implements Demat 2.0 architecture and broker compliance norms
  2. Platforms tokenise corporate debt instruments for exchange-like secondary trading
  3. Smaller denominations enable retail participants to trade corporate debt easily
  4. Enhanced bond liquidity helps corporate issuers raise debt capital more efficiently

The process begins with SEBI issuing the technical and legal framework for Demat 2.0. Over subsequent months, depositories and intermediaries build the infrastructure required to issue and trade tokenised corporate debt. Once operational, fractionalisation allows retail participants to buy into bonds that previously required high minimum capital, gradually improving secondary liquidity across corporate credit over several quarters.

The bull case

What optimists are arguing

Bulls argue that tokenisation will finally democratise India's corporate debt market, creating deep secondary liquidity while graded compliance protects smaller brokerages from excessive regulatory costs.

Depends on:

  • rapid operational adoption by depositories and exchanges
  • strong retail demand for fixed-income products over bank deposits

The bear case

What pessimists are arguing

Market sceptics contend that tokenised structures may introduce unforeseen operational and custodial risks without necessarily generating sustainable retail trading volumes.

Depends on:

  • persistent retail preference for traditional fixed-income avenues like fixed deposits
  • operational friction during the migration to Demat 2.0 systems

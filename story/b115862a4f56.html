The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. Step 1: US jobs and CPI data are released
  2. Step 2: Stronger numbers push expectations of Fed rate hikes
  3. Step 3: Anticipated hikes lift US Treasury yields and strengthen the dollar
  4. Step 4: Higher yields raise funding costs for Indian firms with dollar debt and affect export‑oriented earnings

When the payrolls report shows solid job growth and CPI remains above target, markets price in a higher probability of Fed tightening. That expectation lifts Treasury yields, which in turn makes the dollar more attractive, appreciating its value against the rupee. Indian companies that borrow in dollars face higher interest expenses, while exporters see mixed effects: a stronger dollar can boost foreign‑currency earnings but also dampen overseas demand if global growth slows. The earnings impact typically appears in quarterly results, whereas funding‑cost changes affect cash‑flow immediately.

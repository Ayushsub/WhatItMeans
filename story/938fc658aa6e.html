AU Small Finance Bank Q2 net profit surges 53% to ₹858 crore

Bad loan provisions dropped to ₹359 crore as asset quality improved significantly.

  • AU Small Finance Bank net profit grew 53% year-on-year to ₹858 crore.
  • Lower bad-debt provisioning and strong income growth expanded the lender's quarterly margins.
  • Gross non-performing asset trajectories over coming quarters will test credit sustainability.
Banking & FinancialsInterest RatesCredit & Liquidity

What happened

AU Small Finance Bank reported a 53% year-on-year rise in net profit to ₹858 crore for the second quarter ending September 2026. Total income increased to ₹6,382 crore from ₹5,224 crore in the corresponding quarter last year. The bank's gross non-performing assets ratio declined by 42 basis points to 1.99%, allowing it to reduce quarterly credit provisions by 25% to ₹359 crore.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. Asset quality metrics improve as gross bad loans fall to 1.99%
  2. Lower defaults require fewer provisioning reserves set aside from operating income
  3. Provisions drop 25% to ₹359 crore, preserving more revenue
  4. Net profit expands 53% directly to ₹858 crore

When fewer borrowers default on existing loans, a lender's gross non-performing asset ratio drops. This improvement immediately reduces the mandatory capital the bank must set aside as provisions against potential bad debts. Lower provisioning costs mean a smaller deduction from operating profit within the quarter. Consequently, a higher proportion of top-line revenue flows down to net profit, reflecting in reported quarterly earnings.

Jargon, in plain words

Non-performing assets (NPAs)
Loans where the borrower has stopped making scheduled interest or principal payments for a specified period, typically 90 days.
Provisions
Funds a bank must set aside from its profits to cover anticipated future losses on bad loans.
Basis points (bps)
A standard unit of measure for percentages in finance, where one basis point equals 0.01%.

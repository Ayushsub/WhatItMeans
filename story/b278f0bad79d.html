US imposes 123% anti-dumping and 126% countervailing duties on Indian solar cells

Indian solar exporters face over 120% tariffs on US shipments

What happened

On Sept 12, 2026, the US Commerce Department finalized anti‑dumping duties of 123.04% and countervailing duties of 126.09% on solar cells and panels imported from India, Indonesia and Laos. The measures target alleged unfair subsidies and price dumping that harm US manufacturers.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. US Commerce Department imposes 123% anti‑dumping and 126% countervailing duties on Indian solar imports
  2. Indian exporters must either absorb the duty cost or raise overseas prices
  3. Higher landed costs reduce competitiveness of Indian panels in the US market
  4. Resulting drop in export orders lowers manufacturers' revenue and may compress earnings

The duties act as a price shock that forces Indian exporters to choose between absorbing a large tax or passing it on. If passed on, the price gap makes Indian panels less attractive compared with US‑made alternatives, leading buyers to cut orders. The reduced order flow translates into lower sales and tighter profit margins for manufacturers, a lag that typically unfolds over several months as contracts are renegotiated and inventory is cleared.

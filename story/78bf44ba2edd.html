Jaishankar to visit Russia as Trump threatens 100% secondary tariffs

India's access to discounted Russian crude oil faces a 100% US tariff threat.

  • India's External Affairs Minister is visiting Russia amid US tariff threats.
  • Secondary US tariffs could disrupt India's cheap energy imports and bilateral trade.
  • Watch for diplomatic negotiations and potential exemptions or alternative payment mechanisms.

What happened

India's External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar is scheduled to visit Russia following recent trade threats from the US. US President Donald Trump has threatened to impose a 100% secondary tariff on nations purchasing oil, gas, and uranium from Russia. The visit, reported on August 22, 2026, comes as India seeks to balance its strategic energy ties with Russia against US trade relations.

Who is affected

Indian public sector oil refiners
They face the risk of higher raw material costs if US secondary tariffs force them to reduce cheap Russian crude imports.
Indian exporters to the US
They could face severe margin pressure or loss of competitiveness if the US implements the threatened 100% tariff on Indian goods.

Which sectors

  • Oil and Gas headwind — A potential reduction in Russian crude imports would force refiners to buy more expensive alternatives, squeezing refining margins.
  • Exporters to the US headwind — Any broad-based secondary tariffs imposed by the US would make Indian exports prohibitively expensive in their largest market.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. US threatens 100% secondary tariffs on buyers of Russian energy
  2. Indian refiners face the choice of risking US market access or stopping Russian oil imports
  3. Shifting away from Russian crude increases average crude procurement costs for Indian refiners
  4. Higher input costs squeeze profit margins of domestic oil marketing companies and pressure retail fuel prices

The chain begins with the US announcing secondary tariffs, which immediately creates diplomatic and compliance uncertainty for Indian policymakers. Within weeks, Indian refiners must evaluate their exposure to US financial systems and trade routes, potentially slowing down new purchase contracts with Russia. Over the next few quarters, if refiners substitute discounted Russian crude with pricier Middle Eastern alternatives, their gross refining margins will shrink. Ultimately, this cost escalation is either absorbed by state-run oil marketing companies, hurting their earnings, or passed on to consumers, raising domestic inflation.

Jargon, in plain words

Secondary tariff
A penalty tax imposed by one country (like the US) on a second country (like India) for doing business with a targeted third country (like Russia).
Gross refining margin
The difference between the value of the petroleum products a refinery produces and the cost of the crude oil used to make them.

Analysts warn of tough october as US yields top 5% and rupee weakens

Eight weeks of Nifty correction coincide with US yields over 5%

  • Nifty has been correcting for eight weeks
  • Global factors are pressuring Indian markets
  • Watch US yields, rupee and crude prices
energyfinancialsconsumer discretionaryCurrency

What happened

Analysts note that US Treasury yields have risen above 5%, keeping the dollar firm. At the same time, crude oil prices have climbed and the rupee has weakened against the dollar. These global headwinds come as the Nifty index has been in a correction for about eight weeks, prompting concerns about a painful October.

  • consumer discretionary headwind — inflation erodes disposable income

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. US yields rise above 5%
  2. stronger dollar forces rupee depreciation
  3. depreciated rupee raises import and crude costs
  4. higher costs squeeze corporate margins and lift inflation

Step 1: US Treasury yields climbing above 5% makes dollar‑denominated assets more attractive, pushing the dollar higher. Step 2: A stronger dollar puts pressure on the rupee, causing it to weaken. Step 3: A weaker rupee makes imports, especially crude oil, more expensive for Indian firms. Step 4: Elevated input costs compress profit margins and feed into consumer price inflation, which can weigh on corporate earnings and the Nifty. The lag between each step can span weeks to a few months.

What optimists are arguing

Optimists argue that higher US yields could attract foreign portfolio inflows into Indian equities, supporting the Nifty despite currency pressure.

Depends on:

  • sustained foreign capital inflows despite rupee weakness

What pessimists are arguing

Pessimists contend that a weaker rupee and rising crude prices will erode corporate earnings and fuel inflation, deepening the market correction.

Depends on:

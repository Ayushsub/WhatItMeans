The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: government maintains a freeze on retail fuel prices despite rising crude costs
  2. step 2: BPCL is forced to sell petrol, diesel and LPG at lower-than‑cost levels, compressing marketing margins
  3. step 3: compressed margins lower earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation (EBITDA) and net profit
  4. step 4: reduced profitability appears in the Q2 FY27 financial statements

The price freeze acts as the immediate trigger, pushing BPCL to sell fuel at regulated rates. This forces the company to absorb higher input costs, which squeezes its marketing margins. The margin squeeze then drags down EBITDA and net profit, which are reflected in the quarterly results. The effect typically unfolds over a quarter as pricing policies and input cost changes materialise.

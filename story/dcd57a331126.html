SBI raises MCLR lending rates by 15 basis points after RBI policy hike

A 15-basis-point increase directly touches five distinct tenors of existing SBI borrowers.

  • SBI raised marginal cost of funds-based lending rates by 15 basis points.
  • Borrowing costs rise for corporate and legacy retail loans linked to MCLR.
  • Watch whether competing lenders follow and if credit growth momentum slows down.
BankingCapital-intensive industriesInterest Rates

What happened

State Bank of India increased its marginal cost of funds-based lending rates by 15 basis points across five tenors. The adjustment follows the Reserve Bank of India raising the benchmark repo rate by 25 basis points from 5.25% to 5.50% on October 16, 2026.

Who is affected

existing SBI borrowers on MCLR contracts
Their loan interest rates will rise by 0.15% at their next scheduled rate reset date, increasing interest outgo.
corporate borrowers seeking fresh credit
New loans and working capital lines linked to SBI's MCLR benchmarks will carry immediately higher borrowing costs.

Which sectors

  • Banking tailwind — Lending yields adjust upward ahead of deposit costs, offering near-term support to net interest margins.
  • Capital-intensive industries headwind — Higher benchmark rates raise corporate debt servicing costs and lift the hurdle rate for new investments.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. RBI raises the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50%
  2. SBI reflects higher funding costs by raising MCLR by 15 basis points across five tenors
  3. Contractual interest rates reset upward on linked commercial and retail loans
  4. Higher debt-service costs reduce corporate cash flows and borrower disposable income

The sequence begins when the RBI raises the policy rate, lifting short-term borrowing costs for commercial banks. Within days, SBI adjusted its internal lending benchmark, the MCLR, upward by 15 basis points. Over the following months, loans tied to these tenors reprice on their contractual reset dates. This ultimately translates into higher quarterly finance expenses for businesses and larger monthly payouts for borrowers.

What optimists are arguing

Bulls argue that proactive rate increases help SBI protect net interest margins before deposit cost pressures catch up.

Depends on:

  • Deposit rates rising more slowly than asset yields
  • Sustained credit demand despite higher borrowing expenses

The bear case rests on the view that successive rate hikes may curb credit demand and elevate repayment strains among borrowers.

Jargon, in plain words

MCLR
Marginal Cost of Funds-based Lending Rate: an internal benchmark below which a bank cannot lend, tied to its cost of obtaining fresh funds.
Repo rate
The interest rate at which the Reserve Bank of India lends short-term funds to commercial banks.
Basis point
A financial unit of measurement equal to 0.01 percentage point; 15 basis points equals 0.15%.

SEBI reviews issue proceeds reporting and moves to stop duplicate fines

Dual-listed firms face streamlined penalties under a planned regulatory overhaul.

  • SEBI is reviewing disclosure rules on how companies spend issue proceeds.
  • The regulator also plans to eliminate duplicate penalties across multiple exchanges.
  • Official consultation papers and implementation guidelines from SEBI.
Stock exchanges and financial intermediaries

What happened

On August 22, 2026, the SEBI Chairman announced that the regulator is reviewing the disclosure framework for monitoring how companies deploy proceeds raised from capital issues. Additionally, SEBI is proposing a mechanism to prevent multiple stock exchanges from levying duplicate fines on dual-listed companies for the identical compliance violation.

Who is affected

Companies listed on multiple stock exchanges
Reduced administrative friction and lower compliance penalties by eliminating redundant fines for single infractions.
Public market investors
Greater visibility and clearer tracking of how newly raised capital is actually spent by issuing firms.

Which sectors

  • Stock exchanges and financial intermediaries neutral — Exchanges will need to coordinate enforcement actions to avoid overlapping penalty collections.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. SEBI identifies regulatory overlap and disclosure gaps
  2. Regulator proposes harmonised fine structures and updated reporting norms
  3. Stock exchanges align penalty systems to prevent dual enforcement
  4. Dual-listed corporate issuers experience lower redundant compliance costs

When a company lists on both the BSE and NSE, a single reporting lapse currently risks triggering separate penalties from each bourse. SEBI's proposed framework coordinates exchange oversight so that one consolidated fine applies per violation. Over weeks to months, this reform, paired with updated disclosure rules on fund utilisation, aims to standardise compliance enforcement and enhance transparency for public issues.

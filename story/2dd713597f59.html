India plans ₹15,000 crore LNG storage boost funded by pipeline surcharge

A ₹15,000 crore investment aims to expand India's 12-day fuel buffer.

  • India plans a ₹15,000 crore LNG storage expansion funded via pipeline surcharges.
  • Expanding strategic reserves cushions domestic users against global gas price shocks.
  • Final approval and structure of the pipeline tariff surcharge remain key.
Oil & GasFertilizers & ChemicalsTrade & Tariffs

What happened

India is planning an investment of ₹15,000 crore to expand liquefied natural gas (LNG) storage capacity, according to a Mint report on September 26, 2026. The new infrastructure will add to the country's existing 12-day reserve distributed across more than 20 tanks. To fund the project, the government plans to introduce a surcharge on pipeline transportation tariffs.

Plays out over quarters

  2. Pipeline tariff surcharge implemented to fund project investment
  3. Gas transporters pass additional surcharge into delivered industrial tariffs
  4. Expanded buffer mitigates spot price shocks across the domestic market

The capital outlay for storage tanks initiates multi-year construction cycles across domestic engineering and gas infrastructure firms. To finance this, regulators levy a dedicated surcharge onto existing pipeline transportation fees within coming quarters. Downstream buyers absorb these higher delivery costs, but gain protection against spot market supply interruptions as storage capacity expands.

India's basmati rice yield expected to rise 5% to 10% on good monsoon

Favourable monsoon rains could boost India's basmati rice yield by 10 percent.

  • India's basmati rice yield is expected to increase by 5% to 10% this year.
  • Higher crop yields expand exportable surpluses for rice exporters and stabilize domestic availability.
  • Export demand trends and paddy procurement prices in coming harvest months.
Agriculture & AgribusinessTrade & Tariffs

What happened

On October 24, 2026, Basmati Rice Farmers and Exporters Association (IREF) President Rajeev Setia stated that India's basmati rice yield is likely to rise 5% to 10% this year. The projected growth in harvest volume is attributed to supportive monsoon rainfall throughout the crop growing cycle. Setia, who also serves as Joint Managing Director of Chaman Lal Setia Exports, noted that weather conditions boosted crop health across major growing belts.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. step 1: Favourable monsoon rainfall improves soil moisture and crop productivity.
  2. step 2: Basmati rice harvest yields increase by 5% to 10% per acre.
  3. step 3: Higher paddy supply enters wholesale mandis during the harvest season.
  4. step 4: Exporters procure larger quantities to fulfill international shipments.

Favourable monsoon weather directly enhances crop productivity during the cultivation phase. Within weeks of harvest, increased paddy arrivals reach wholesale markets, boosting available inventory for processors. Exporters and millers process this grain over subsequent months, translating higher volumes into export contracts. The financial impact shows up in earnings over one to two quarters as shipments are fulfilled.

What optimists are arguing

Bulls argue that higher yields will allow Indian exporters to capture market share globally while managing raw material procurement costs.

What pessimists are arguing

Bears contend that an influx of paddy supply could depress market realization prices if overseas export demand fails to keep pace.

Depends on:

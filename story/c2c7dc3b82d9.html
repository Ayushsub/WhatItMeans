August CPI hits 4.8%, reviving RBI rate‑hike expectations

Retail price surge to 4.8% in August fuels speculation on RBI policy

  • August CPI rose to 4.8%.
  • Higher inflation may prompt RBI to raise rates.

What happened

The Consumer Price Index for August climbed to 4.8%, a 20‑month high, mainly driven by rising food prices. Economists said the jump raises the probability that the Reserve Bank of India will tighten policy in its October meeting. The RBI has not yet announced any change to its repo rate.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: August CPI climbs to 4.8%
  2. step 2: RBI is likely to lift the repo rate in October
  3. step 3: Higher repo rate pushes up lending and deposit rates
  4. step 4: Companies face higher financing costs and consumers face larger loan repayments

The CPI surge forces the RBI to consider a rate hike to contain price pressures. A higher repo rate makes borrowing more expensive for banks, which pass the cost onto borrowers and raise deposit rates for savers. The cost increase appears in corporate earnings as higher interest expense and in consumer spending as reduced disposable income. The lag is typically a few weeks for banks to adjust rates and a quarter for earnings to reflect the change.

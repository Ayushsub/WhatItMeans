SEBI launches Demat 2.0 pilot for tokenised corporate bonds

IIFL Finance issues India's first tokenised corporate bond under a new regulatory pilot.

  • SEBI launched a Demat 2.0 pilot to tokenise corporate bonds.
  • It automates interest payments and enables instant settlement for bond investors.
  • Watch the expansion of this pilot to more issuers and secondary market trading.
Financial ServicesInformation TechnologyCredit & Liquidity

What happened

On September 12, 2026, SEBI launched its Demat 2.0 pilot project to enable the tokenisation of corporate bonds. Under this pilot, the Metropolitan Stock Exchange facilitated India's first tokenised corporate bond, which was issued by IIFL Finance Ltd. Trust Investment Advisors Pvt Ltd acted as the arranger for this debut transaction.

Who is affected

Retail and institutional bond investors
They will experience faster settlement times and automated interest payouts, reducing the risk of delayed payments.
Corporate bond issuers
They can raise debt capital more efficiently by streamlining the administrative process of managing bondholders and payouts.
Financial intermediaries and arrangers
They must adapt their technology systems to support tokenised bond issuances and smart contract execution.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. SEBI introduces Demat 2.0 tokenisation for corporate bonds
  2. Smart contracts automate interest payments and redemption processes
  3. Investors receive funds instantly without manual clearing house delays
  4. Lower transaction costs and reduced settlement risk improve liquidity in the corporate bond market

The transition begins with SEBI's pilot, which allows issuers to convert traditional bonds into digital tokens. This shift forces the automation of administrative tasks, as smart contracts execute payouts immediately when due, bypassing traditional banking clearing delays. For investors, this eliminates the typical multi-day lag for settlement and interest distribution, making capital immediately reusable. Over quarters, as more issuers adopt this system, the reduction in operational friction is expected to lower transaction costs and attract more liquidity to the corporate debt market.

Jargon, in plain words

Tokenisation
The process of converting ownership rights of an asset, like a bond, into a digital token on a blockchain or distributed ledger.
Smart contracts
Self-executing digital contracts with the terms of the agreement directly written into lines of code, which automatically run when predetermined conditions are met.
Atomic settlement
A settlement mechanism where the transfer of an asset and the transfer of payment happen simultaneously, ensuring one cannot happen without the other.

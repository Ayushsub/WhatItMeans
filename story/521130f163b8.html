India expands subsidised onion sales to small towns as prices double

Retail onion prices have risen 100% year‑on‑year, prompting the subsidy

What happened

On 26 September 2026 the Indian government announced an expansion of its subsidised onion sales programme to district markets and small towns. It will ship buffer‑stock onions from government warehouses to these locations to check price spillovers and curb festive season inflation. Retail onion prices have doubled compared with the same period last year.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. step 1: government releases buffer‑stock onions to district markets and small towns
  2. step 2: local supply rises, easing price pressure on retail onions
  3. step 3: retail onion prices fall, reducing overall food price inflation
  4. step 4: lower inflation eases pressure on RBI to raise repo rate

The policy triggers an immediate increase in physical onion supply at the local level, which forces market prices to soften. As retail prices retreat, household food bills face less upward pressure, feeding into the consumer‑price‑index (CPI) and tempering overall inflation. With inflation easing, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) faces less urgency to tighten monetary policy. The effects on prices appear within weeks, while any impact on monetary stance may take a few months.

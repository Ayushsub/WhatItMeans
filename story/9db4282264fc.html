GST council proposes 2% tds on scrap transactions

Registered buyers will need to withhold tax on plastic waste purchases

  • GST council proposes 2% tds on scrap deals
  • Adds compliance cost for buyers and sellers in waste recycling
  • Watch for final approval and sector response
waste recycling and processingplastics and tyre manufacturersFiscal & Budget

What happened

On Oct 9 2026 the GST Council announced a proposal to levy a 2% tax deducted at source (tds) on scrap transactions. The levy would apply to plastic waste, discarded tyres, electrical and electronic scrap and used cooking oil. Registered buyers must deduct tds from payments to registered suppliers and pay GST under the reverse charge mechanism for purchases from unregistered sellers.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: GST council proposes 2% tds on scrap deals
  2. step 2: buyers must deduct tds and handle reverse‑charge gst, raising transaction cost
  3. step 3: higher costs reduce profit margins for recyclers or are passed to downstream users
  4. step 4: earnings reflect lower margins or prices of recycled material rise

The proposal (step 1) forces buyers to withhold 2% of the invoice amount (step 2), which reduces cash available to suppliers and adds an administrative step. Companies in the recycling chain either absorb the cost, squeezing margins, or increase prices for downstream manufacturers (step 3). The effect shows up in quarterly earnings as lower profitability or in market prices as higher recycled‑material rates. The lag is typically a few weeks for compliance set‑up and a quarter for earnings impact.

RBI and finance ministry urge banks to maintain ATM cash ahead of UPI charges

Banks face pressure to stock ATMs as UPI charges loom

  • RBI and finance ministry ask banks to keep ATM cash
  • Potential UPI fees could push users back to cash
  • Watch ATM cash levels and UPI fee rollout
banking

What happened

On September 26, 2026 the Reserve Bank of India and the Finance Ministry asked banks to ensure sufficient cash in their ATM networks ahead of the introduction of merchant discount rate (MDR) charges on UPI transactions. The guidance comes as banks' ATM footprints have shrunk over recent years while digital payments continue to grow. The aim is to pre‑empt a shift back to cash usage once the new fees are applied.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. step 1: upcoming UPI merchant discount rate (MDR) charges are announced
  2. step 2: banks anticipate reduced digital transaction volumes and possible cash‑seeking behaviour
  3. step 3: banks are forced to keep larger cash balances in ATMs and increase replenishment frequency
  4. step 4: higher cash‑handling costs appear in banks' expense lines, potentially squeezing margins

The MDR announcement (step 1) creates an expectation that some users will revert to cash (step 2). To avoid cash shortages, banks must stock more notes in ATMs (step 3), which raises logistics and security expenses. Those costs materialise in the banks' quarterly earnings as higher operating expenses, affecting profitability. The lag between the policy announcement and expense impact typically spans a few weeks to a couple of months.

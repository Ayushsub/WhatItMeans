US treasury official predicts oil could fall to $40 after Iran war, easing yields

India's oil import bill could shrink as crude slides toward $40 per barrel

What happened

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said oil could drop to $40 a barrel once the Iran conflict ends. He linked the expected price fall to an oversupply scenario and said it would pull down bond yields that have recently been at multi‑year highs. The comments were reported on 4‑5 September 2026.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: the Iran war ends, creating an oversupply of crude
  2. step 2: global oil price slides toward $40 per barrel
  3. step 3: cheaper oil reduces import costs and eases inflation pressure in India
  4. step 4: lower inflation allows bond yields to fall, cutting corporate borrowing costs

When the conflict stops, producers keep output high, flooding the market and pushing prices down. The price drop cuts the cost of imported crude for Indian refiners, which feeds through to lower fuel prices for households and firms. With inflationary pressure easing, the Reserve Bank of India may keep policy rates steady, and global bond yields retreat, improving financing terms for Indian companies. The lag from price change to consumer bills is a few weeks, while the impact on yields may take a month or two.

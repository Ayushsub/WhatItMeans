GIFT IFSC expands global banking footprint with offshore fundraising

Indian businesses secured $54.2 billion in foreign capital through a single specialized financial hub.

  • GIFT IFSC raised $54.2 billion in overseas funds following regulatory approvals.
  • It provides Indian firms with cheaper, direct access to international debt and equity.
  • The pace of regulatory easing and future foreign capital inflows into the hub.
Banking & FinancialsInfrastructure & Real Estate

What happened

The Gujarat International Finance Tec-City (GIFT) International Financial Services Centre (IFSC) has raised approximately $54.2 billion in overseas funds. This milestone follows the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) permitting Indian entities to seek capital abroad through this offshore hub. The development positions the financial center as a key intermediary for funding India's long-term infrastructure and development goals.

Who is affected

large Indian corporates seeking foreign debt
They gain access to a larger pool of global liquidity, potentially lowering their borrowing costs.
international banks operating in GIFT IFSC
They experience increased transaction volumes and a wider client base for underwriting and advisory services.

Which sectors

  • Banking & Financials tailwind — Increased offshore fundraising activity boosts fee-based income and asset books for banks operating within the IFSC.
  • Infrastructure & Real Estate tailwind — These capital-intensive sectors gain an alternative, streamlined channel to secure large-scale foreign investments.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. RBI permits Indian entities to raise capital via GIFT IFSC
  2. Global investors pool foreign currency funds into the offshore hub
  3. Indian companies issue debt or equity instruments directly within the IFSC
  4. Capital is deployed into domestic projects, reducing reliance on local bank credit

The transmission begins with regulatory clearance, allowing Indian firms to bypass traditional offshore jurisdictions. Global investors channel foreign currency into GIFT IFSC banks, which then underwrite or facilitate debt and equity issuances for Indian corporates. This process typically takes several months to structure and execute. Ultimately, the inflow of cheaper foreign capital reduces the demand pressure on domestic banking liquidity, potentially softening local corporate borrowing rates over quarters.

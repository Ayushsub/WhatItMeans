Raising PSB foreign investment cap to 49% could trigger Rs 30,000 crore inflows

State Bank of India could see Rs 20,000 crore in foreign buying.

  • Public sector banks requested raising foreign investment caps from 20% to 49%.
  • Higher headroom could trigger up to Rs 30,000 crore in passive foreign funds.
  • Government approval and subsequent global equity index weight revisions will dictate outcomes.
BankingInstitutional Flows

What happened

On November 6, 2026, public sector banks requested the Indian government to increase the foreign investment limit in state-owned lenders from 20% to 49%. Lenders estimate that this regulatory change could trigger passive foreign capital inflows of at least Rs 30,000 crore into public sector banks. State Bank of India alone is projected to receive around Rs 20,000 crore due to expected weightage adjustments in global stock indices.

Who is affected

Public sector bank shareholders
State Bank of India and other public lenders could see higher share demand from foreign institutional investors.
Foreign portfolio investors
Passive global index funds gain structural room to increase allocations toward Indian state-owned banks.

Which sectors

  • Banking tailwind — Higher foreign investment headroom enables up to Rs 30,000 crore in passive foreign equity inflows into public sector lenders.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. Government raises foreign investment limit for public sector banks from 20% to 49%
  2. Global stock index providers increase the foreign inclusion factor for state-owned banks
  3. Passive index-tracking foreign funds automatically buy bank equity to align with updated index weights
  4. Institutional demand injects liquidity and places upward pressure on public bank valuations

If the government approves increasing the foreign direct investment limit from 20% to 49%, available headroom for foreign investors expands. Global benchmark providers would then adjust the foreign inclusion weight of public sector banks over several months. Passive index-tracking funds are required to buy the underlying shares to mirror these updated weights, creating structured foreign inflows over subsequent quarters.

Jargon, in plain words

Passive foreign inflows
Capital invested automatically by foreign funds tracking stock indices, rather than through active individual stock selection.
Foreign investment cap
The legal upper limit on the percentage of a domestic company's equity that overseas investors are permitted to own.

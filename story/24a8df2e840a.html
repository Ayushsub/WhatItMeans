Bank of Korea signals caution after split vote on August interest rate hike

Rising loan delinquencies are challenging the Bank of Korea's latest interest rate hike.

  • The Bank of Korea raised interest rates in August despite a dissenting vote.
  • Rising loan delinquencies and slowing growth are making further rate hikes highly unlikely.
  • Watch South Korean delinquency rates and inflation prints for signs of a pause.
Banking & FinancialsInterest RatesInflationGrowth

What happened

During its August 2026 meeting, the Bank of Korea raised its benchmark interest rate, but the decision was split with a 1-member dissent. One policymaker voted against the hike, advocating instead for a pause in the tightening cycle. This dissent highlights growing internal concern over rising loan delinquencies and slowing economic growth despite persistent inflation risks.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. Bank of Korea raises the benchmark interest rate
  2. Commercial banks increase lending rates for consumers and businesses
  3. Borrowers face higher debt servicing costs amid slowing economic growth
  4. Loan delinquencies rise, forcing banks to increase provisions for bad loans

When a central bank raises rates, commercial banks typically pass on these higher costs to borrowers within a lag of 2 to 4 weeks. For highly leveraged households and businesses, this immediately increases the burden of servicing existing variable-rate debt. Over a lag of 1 to 2 quarters, these elevated costs pressure cash flows, leading to a rise in loan defaults and delinquencies. Ultimately, this shows up on commercial banks' balance sheets as higher non-performing assets, dampening their profitability.

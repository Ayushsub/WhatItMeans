Sensex slides 548 points as crude oil spikes and foreign funds sell

An intraday swing of 796 points erased early equity gains across Mumbai trading.

  • The BSE Sensex dropped 548 points to close at 71,924.68 after morning gains.
  • Surging crude prices and foreign institutional selling pressure domestic macroeconomic stability and margins.
  • Global crude oil price movements and daily net foreign portfolio investment flows.

What happened

On October 17, 2026, the 30-share BSE Sensex dropped 547.65 points, or 0.76%, to settle at 71,924.68. The index reversed earlier morning gains and sank up to 796.23 points, or 1%, to an intraday low of 71,676.10. The downturn was triggered by a sudden surge in international crude oil prices accompanied by foreign institutional outflows.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over days

  1. Global crude oil prices experience a sudden upward spike
  2. India's projected import bill rises, triggering macroeconomic concerns around inflation and currency stability
  3. Foreign institutional investors liquidate emerging market equity holdings to de-risk
  4. Benchmark indices decline as large-cap institutional selling overwhelms intraday buying

A sharp rise in global crude prices immediately raises concerns over India's trade deficit and imported inflation within days. Seeing elevated macro risks, foreign institutional investors sell liquid large-cap shares to reduce risk. This concentrated selling pressure drains market liquidity over the trading session, causing benchmark indices like the Sensex to reverse morning advances.

Market reaction

Erasing opening gains, the 30-share BSE Sensex tumbled 547.65 points, or 0.76%, to settle at 71,924.68, having slumped 796.23 points, or 1%, to an intraday low of 71,676.10.

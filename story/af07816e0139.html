FPIs pull ₹5,109 crore from Indian government bonds over three days

Global funds pulled ₹5,109 crore out of Indian government debt in mid-September.

  • Foreign investors withdrew ₹5,109 crore from Indian government bonds over three days.
  • Outflows put downward pressure on the rupee and raise government borrowing costs.
  • Watch movements in US Treasury yields and global crude oil prices.
Banking and Financial ServicesOil Marketing CompaniesCurrencyCrude & EnergyInstitutional Flows

What happened

Foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) withdrew ₹5,109 crore from Indian government securities under the Fully Accessible Route (FAR) between September 9 and September 15, 2026. According to Clearing Corporation of India (CCIL) data, total FPI holdings in these bonds fell from ₹371,915.496 crore to ₹366,806.286 crore. This selling pressure was triggered by rising US Treasury yields, a weakening rupee, and climbing global crude oil prices.

Indian government

Importers paying in US dollars

Which sectors

  • Banking and Financial Services headwind — Falling bond prices lead to mark-to-market treasury losses on banks' existing government bond portfolios.
  • Oil Marketing Companies headwind — Rising crude oil prices combined with a weaker rupee increase the cost of importing raw crude, squeezing margins.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. US Treasury yields and global crude oil prices rise
  2. Foreign investors pull capital out of Indian government bonds to seek safer, higher-yielding US assets
  3. FPIs convert their rupee proceeds to US dollars, putting downward pressure on the Indian rupee
  4. A weaker rupee and selling pressure force Indian bond yields higher, increasing overall domestic borrowing costs

When US Treasury yields rise, the yield spread between Indian and US debt narrows, prompting foreign investors to relocate capital to safer US assets almost immediately. As these investors sell Indian bonds, they convert rupees to dollars, which depreciates the rupee within days. This currency depreciation, coupled with rising crude oil prices, fuels domestic inflation expectations. Consequently, domestic bond yields rise over subsequent weeks, making it more expensive for the Indian government and corporates to raise new debt.

Market reaction

The source reporting notes that foreign portfolio investment in government securities under the Fully Accessible Route (FAR) dropped to ₹366,806.286 crore on September 15 from ₹371,915.496 crore on September 9, reflecting a net outflow of ₹5,109 crore.

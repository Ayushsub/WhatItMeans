Smaller small-cap mutual funds outperform large peers amid AUM drag

Negative one-year returns in mega funds highlight the cost of bloated asset sizes.

  • Large small-cap mutual funds recorded negative one-year returns while smaller rivals outperformed.
  • Swelling asset sizes make trading illiquid smaller company shares difficult without impacting prices.
  • Fund portfolio disclosures and cash allocation levels across small-cap fund categories.
Asset managementCredit & Liquidity

What happened

Small-cap mutual funds managing the largest asset pools have recorded negative one-year returns, according to analysis reported on October 24, 2026. Conversely, several schemes with smaller asset bases generated higher returns over the same period. Market analysts attribute the divergence primarily to liquidity constraints and the operational difficulty of deploying large sums into low-volume small-cap equities.

Who is affected

Investors in mega-sized small-cap funds
Facing negative one-year returns as large asset bases limit managers' ability to trade swiftly.
Investors in boutique or smaller small-cap funds
Benefiting from relative outperformance driven by faster portfolio reallocation into nimble opportunities.

Which sectors

  • Asset management mixed — Larger assets under management generate higher fee income, but sustained underperformance risks triggering investor redemptions.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. Heavy investor inflows expand a small-cap fund's total assets under management
  2. Manager must deploy large cash piles without breaching regulatory concentration limits
  3. Low trading volume in small firms forces the fund to either move share prices unfavourably or buy larger, slower-moving companies
  4. Portfolio returns fall behind nimbler peers with smaller asset bases

When retail money pours into a small-cap fund, the asset manager must invest that capital into underlying businesses. Because small-cap equities have relatively low daily trading volumes, buying or selling large quantities pushes prices against the fund over several weeks or months. Consequently, managers of bloated funds either dilute their high-conviction ideas by holding dozens of extra stocks or park capital in large-cap equities and cash, directly weighing on annual returns.

The bull case

What optimists are arguing

Bulls argue that larger small-cap funds operate with stricter liquidity filters and governance checks, which protects investor capital from severe downside when smaller unproven businesses face financial distress.

Depends on:

  • Earnings stability in established small-cap and mid-cap holdings
  • A shift in market breadth favouring higher-quality, institutional-grade smaller companies

The bear case

What pessimists are arguing

The bear case rests on asset bloat becoming a structural performance drag, forcing large schemes to effectively mimic broad market indices while charging active management fees.

Depends on:

  • Persistent illiquidity in secondary equity markets preventing rapid position changes
  • Ongoing retail inflows that further inflate the asset sizes of top schemes

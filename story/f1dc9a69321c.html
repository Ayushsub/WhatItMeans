FPI return to Indian equities is tactical rather than structural, says HSBC Mutual Fund

Foreign portfolio investors are returning to Indian equities but demand stronger corporate earnings first.

  • Foreign portfolio investors are cautiously returning to Indian stock markets.
  • The inflows are short-term tactical moves rather than long-term structural shifts.
  • Future corporate earnings growth and stock valuations must sustain these inflows.
ManufacturingLarge-cap EquitiesInstitutional Flows

What happened

On August 22, 2026, HSBC Mutual Fund's Venugopal Manghat stated that the recent return of foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) to Indian equities appears tactical rather than structural. While India benefits from stable domestic demand and manufacturing growth, sustained foreign inflows remain unconfirmed. FPIs are currently exercising valuation discipline as they monitor corporate earnings.

Who is affected

retail investors in Indian equities
They may experience short-term market volatility if foreign inflows quickly reverse due to high valuations.
large-cap Indian companies
They could see temporary stock price support from tactical foreign buying, but this support remains fragile without earnings growth.

Which sectors

  • Manufacturing tailwind — Stable domestic demand and structural growth in manufacturing continue to attract cautious foreign interest.
  • Large-cap Equities mixed — While they benefit from tactical foreign inflows, high valuations keep foreign investors cautious about long-term allocations.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. Foreign portfolio investors identify short-term tactical opportunities in Indian equities
  2. Capital flows into large-cap stocks, temporarily boosting liquidity and valuations
  3. High valuations pressure corporate earnings to justify the elevated stock prices
  4. If earnings growth lags, foreign investors withdraw capital to maintain valuation discipline

When global market dynamics shift, foreign portfolio investors deploy capital into Indian equities as a short-term tactical play, which immediately boosts market liquidity. This initial inflow typically reflects in stock prices within days. However, for this capital to stay long-term, corporate earnings must grow to justify these higher valuations, a process that plays out over several quarters. If corporate earnings fail to match expectations, valuation-conscious foreign funds will likely pull back their capital.

What pessimists are arguing

Jargon, in plain words

Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs)
Foreign individuals or institutions investing in India's financial assets, such as stocks and bonds.
Tactical Reallocation
A short-term investment strategy that temporarily shifts assets to take advantage of market anomalies or strong sectors.
Structural Reallocation
A long-term, permanent shift in asset allocation based on fundamental changes in an economy or market.

Advisors outline asset allocation strategies for NRIs investing in India

Non-Resident Indians face complex choices when deploying a Rs 1 crore portfolio.

  • An investment advisor outlined asset allocation frameworks for Non-Resident Indians.
  • NRIs must balance global portfolios, tax implications, and repatriation needs before investing.
  • How changing tax laws and currency fluctuations affect NRI investment flows into India.

What happened

An investment advisor outlined asset allocation frameworks for Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) looking to invest in India over a five-to-seven-year horizon. The guidance emphasizes evaluating global portfolios, existing Indian exposure, liquidity needs, tax implications, and repatriation rules before committing capital. An illustrative multi-asset model was suggested to balance risk across equities, fixed income, gold, and alternative assets.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. NRIs evaluate their global asset exposure and repatriation needs
  2. Capital is allocated across Indian equities, debt, and gold based on risk appetite
  3. Inflows increase liquidity in Indian financial markets
  4. Asset management companies experience growth in assets under management

When Non-Resident Indians decide to invest in India, they must first assess their domestic tax status and repatriation limits, which typically takes weeks to resolve. Once cleared, capital flows into Indian equities, bonds, and gold, directly increasing the liquidity of these asset classes. This steady inflow of foreign capital provides a tailwind to Indian asset management companies, whose fee revenues grow in proportion to their rising assets under management. The full impact of these capital allocations typically reflects in corporate earnings and market depth over several quarters.

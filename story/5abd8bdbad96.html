Rupee falls to 95.55 as crude oil climbs to 105 dollars amid geopolitical tensions

Importers face higher costs after the currency slipped eleven paise on Friday.

  • The Indian rupee fell eleven paise to close at 95.55 against the US dollar.
  • Costlier crude oil at 105 dollars per barrel increases India's import bill and inflation.
  • Watch geopolitical tensions in the Strait of Hormuz and global crude oil price movements.

What happened

On Friday, September 11, 2026, the Indian rupee depreciated by 11 paise to close at 95.55 against the US dollar. This decline was driven by benchmark crude oil prices rising to $105 per barrel. Geopolitical tensions in the Strait of Hormuz further pressured the domestic currency during the week.

Who is affected

oil importing companies
They will have to shell out more rupees to purchase US dollars for their crude oil shipments, raising their procurement costs.
Indian consumers
They may face higher fuel prices and retail inflation as oil marketing companies pass on the increased cost of imported crude.
Indian exporters
They might see a marginal increase in their rupee realizations when converting their dollar-denominated export earnings.

Which sectors

  • Oil and Gas headwind — Rising crude prices and a weaker rupee increase the cost of raw materials, potentially squeezing refining margins if they cannot pass costs to consumers.
  • Information Technology tailwind — IT companies earn most of their revenue in US dollars, meaning a weaker rupee boosts their profit margins when earnings are converted back.
  • Paint and Chemicals headwind — These industries use crude oil derivatives as key raw materials, and a weaker rupee compounds the impact of rising global oil prices.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. Geopolitical tensions in the Strait of Hormuz disrupt oil supply routes
  2. Global crude oil prices rise to 105 dollars per barrel
  3. India's oil importers demand more US dollars to pay for expensive crude
  4. Increased demand for dollars depreciates the rupee to 95.55
  5. Imported inflation rises, squeezing corporate profit margins and consumer purchasing power

When geopolitical tensions flare up in key transit points like the Strait of Hormuz, oil supply fears immediately push global crude prices higher, as seen with crude reaching $105 per barrel. Because India imports over 80% of its oil, domestic oil companies must purchase more US dollars to pay for these costlier shipments, a process that plays out over days to weeks. This surge in dollar demand weakens the rupee against the greenback, culminating in the 11 paise drop to 95.55. Over the subsequent weeks and months, these higher import costs filter down to the broader economy, raising fuel prices and squeezing corporate margins for industries reliant on oil derivatives.

Jargon, in plain words

Depreciate
A decrease in the value of a currency relative to another currency, meaning it takes more units of the domestic currency to buy one unit of foreign currency.
Benchmark crude
A standard pricing reference point for the global oil market, such as Brent crude, used by buyers and sellers to price oil contracts.

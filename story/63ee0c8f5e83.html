Credit card no‑cost EMI offers hide processing fees and GST, warns Mint

What happened

On 7 November 2026 Mint published a story warning that credit‑card no‑cost EMI schemes for Diwali shopping conceal five hidden costs. The article lists processing fees, GST on the interest component, loss of instant discounts, foreclosure charges and the impact on the cardholder’s credit limit as the main traps, noting that shoppers often review the fine print before converting purchases.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. step 1: banks and card issuers launch no‑cost EMI promotions for festive buying
  2. step 2: they embed processing fees, GST on interest and other charges that are not shown as interest cost
  3. step 3: consumers pay a higher total amount or lose discount benefits, and part of their credit limit is tied up
  4. step 4: the extra cost appears in higher monthly instalments or reduced borrowing capacity, affecting household cash flow

The trigger is the festive‑season push for credit‑card EMI. Because the offers are marketed as ‘no‑cost’, issuers compensate by adding fees that are disclosed only in the fine print. This forces shoppers to either absorb a higher effective price or sacrifice other discounts, which in turn reduces their disposable income or limits future borrowing. The impact shows up in the next billing cycle as larger instalment amounts or a lower available credit limit. The lag is typically one billing period, i.e., a few weeks after the purchase.

Jargon, in plain words

foreclosure charges

The portion of the card's total borrowing capacity that becomes unavailable while the EMI is active

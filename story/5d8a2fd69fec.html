Brent crude jumps 13.6% in October to $117.64 a barrel

India’s fuel import bill spikes as Brent hits $117.64 per barrel

  • Oil prices rose 13.6% in October.
  • Higher crude costs pressure Indian importers and consumers.
  • Watch fuel prices, inflation and RBI policy.
Energy & OilTransportationConsumer GoodsCrude & Energy

What happened

Brent crude rose 13.6% in October, settling at $117.64 a barrel, a six‑month high. The surge was driven by fears that the United States may launch fresh strikes on Iran and by threats to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, raising supply concerns. Analysts warned that further upside was possible if tensions persisted.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. Step 1: Brent crude climbs to $117.64 per barrel
  2. Step 2: India's import bill for crude oil rises sharply
  3. Step 3: Fuel prices for gasoline, diesel and jet fuel increase
  4. Step 4: Higher transport and logistics costs feed into consumer price inflation
  5. Step 5: Companies' earnings face margin pressure while refiners may see higher spreads

The price jump forces importers to spend more on crude, which pushes retail fuel prices up. Higher fuel costs raise operating expenses for transport, logistics and airlines, and these costs are passed on to consumers, feeding into CPI inflation. Refiners may benefit from wider spreads between crude and product prices, but the net effect on earnings depends on the balance between higher input costs and margin gains. The lag from crude price to retail fuel and then to inflation typically spans a few weeks.

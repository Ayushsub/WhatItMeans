GST rule change forces actual tax payment for input credit from nov 1

From Nov 1, GST reforms require suppliers to actually pay declared tax

  • GST filing rule altered
  • ITC now tied to actual tax payment
  • Watch compliance and cash‑flow impacts
manufacturingretailservicesFiscal & Budget

What happened

The Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs confirmed that from November 1, 2026 new GST reforms will be introduced in phases. The amendment mandates that tax declared as payable by a supplier and passed on as input tax credit (ITC) to the recipient must be physically paid by the supplier. This replaces the earlier practice where ITC could be claimed even if the supplier’s tax remained unpaid.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: GST rule requires actual tax payment by supplier
  2. step 2: suppliers must settle GST before passing ITC
  3. step 3: downstream buyers receive credit only after payment, tightening their cash cycles
  4. step 4: earnings may reflect higher financing costs or delayed tax benefits

The rule change forces suppliers to clear their GST liability first (step 1). This compels them to allocate cash to tax payments rather than operating needs (step 2). Downstream firms can claim ITC only after the supplier’s payment clears, so they may need to fund purchases longer (step 3). Over a quarter, companies could see higher working‑capital expenses and a lag in tax‑benefit realization in profit statements (step 4).

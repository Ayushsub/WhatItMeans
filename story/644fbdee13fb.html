Indian banks eye FCNR deposits to fuel 15 percent credit growth in FY27

Indian banks target a 15 percent credit expansion using foreign currency deposits.

  • Indian banks are increasingly using foreign currency deposits to fund credit growth.
  • This strategy is expected to boost net interest income and earnings in FY27.
  • Whether systemic credit growth can sustain the projected 15 percent year-on-year pace.
Banking and Financial ServicesCredit & Liquidity

What happened

According to a report published on August 22, 2026, Indian banks are projecting a systemic credit growth of around 15 percent year-on-year for the financial year 2027. To support this expansion, banks are actively mobilizing Foreign Currency Non-Resident (FCNR) deposits. This influx of foreign currency deposits is expected to drive stronger net interest income and overall earnings growth.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. Banks aggressively mobilize Foreign Currency Non-Resident (FCNR) deposits
  2. This inflow increases the pool of lendable foreign and domestic-converted funds
  3. Banks deploy these funds to meet robust domestic credit demand, targeting 15 percent growth
  4. Higher loan volumes relative to funding costs expand the net interest margin
  5. This expansion directly increases net interest income and overall bank earnings in FY27

The process of targeting a 15 percent credit growth begins when banks raise FCNR deposits from overseas depositors, which typically takes a few weeks to mobilize. Once these foreign currency funds are secured, banks convert or swap them to deploy as credit, a step that responds to market demand over a quarter. This deployment feeds into systemic credit growth, helping banks maintain a healthy loan-to-deposit ratio. Ultimately, the difference between the interest earned on these loans and the interest paid on FCNR deposits widens the net interest margin, showing up as higher net interest income in quarterly earnings reports with a lag of one to two quarters.

Jargon, in plain words

FCNR deposits
Foreign Currency Non-Resident deposits, which are fixed deposits held in foreign currencies by non-resident Indians in Indian banks.
Net interest income
The difference between the interest revenue a bank earns from its lending activities and the interest it pays to its depositors.

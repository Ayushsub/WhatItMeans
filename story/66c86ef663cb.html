Sensex slides 778 points as Brent crude tops $109 and US yields breach 5%

India's total market valuation sank below $5 trillion amid widespread global trading disruptions.

  • Crude topped $109 and US yields hit 5.04%, driving Indian equities to multi-month lows.
  • Costlier oil inflates India's import bill, weakening the rupee and spurring domestic inflation risks.
  • Upcoming US Federal Reserve rate decision and Saudi pipeline repair timelines.

What happened

On September 15, 2026, Brent crude futures jumped above $109 per barrel following drone strikes that shut down Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline alongside Libyan supply halts. Concurrently, the US 10-year Treasury yield surged to 5.041%, its highest mark since July 2007, ahead of an expected Federal Reserve rate move. The dual shock dragged India's BSE Sensex down 777.94 points to 74,003.82 and the NSE Nifty down 279.50 points to 23,118.60, pushing total Indian market capitalization below $5 trillion.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. Middle East infrastructure attacks disrupt oil supply lines, sending Brent past $109
  2. Higher fuel prices fuel inflation expectations, driving US 10-year Treasury yields above 5%
  3. Foreign investors pull capital from emerging markets toward high-yielding US debt, weakening the rupee
  4. Costlier energy imports push India's domestic inflation higher and trigger central bank liquidity tightening

Supply outages in Saudi Arabia and Libya immediately lifted energy costs within hours. Within days, bond markets repriced inflation risk, pushing US Treasury yields to 19-year highs and prompting foreign institutional outflows from Indian equities. Over the coming weeks, a weakened rupee magnifies the cost of imported raw materials across domestic manufacturing. Over quarters, if elevated fuel costs persist, the Reserve Bank of India may face pressure to sustain tighter monetary conditions, raising overall corporate borrowing costs.

Market reaction

The BSE Sensex lost 777.94 points to close at 74,003.82, while the Nifty 50 declined 279.50 points to 23,118.60 with 2,824 NSE stocks falling. The Nifty Smallcap 100 dropped 2.01% and the Midcap 100 slid 1.65%. The Indian rupee weakened to a three-month low, India's 10-year benchmark yield rose 7 basis points to 7.07%, and Wall Street indices closed lower led by drops in the Dow Jones and S&P 500.

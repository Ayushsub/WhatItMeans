LPG under-recovery projected to reach ₹370 per cylinder by December

State refiners face growing losses on cooking gas supplied to 33 crore households.

  • Indian Oil Corporation projects LPG losses reaching ₹370 per cylinder by December 2026.
  • Unrecovered costs squeeze profitability for public sector oil marketing companies.
  • Government fiscal support or potential retail price adjustments in coming quarters.
Oil & Gas RefinersCrude & Energy

What happened

Indian Oil Corporation (IOC) projects under-recoveries on Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) will rise to ₹309 per cylinder in November 2026 and ₹370 per cylinder in December 2026. IOC reported volatile crude procurement costs in the second quarter, fluctuating between discounts and premiums. Cooking fuel sold at below-market rates impacts supply to over 33 crore households across India.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. Volatile global crude and procurement costs increase raw material expenses for LPG.
  2. Retail prices for cooking gas are kept below market rates to protect consumers.
  3. Oil marketing companies absorb the shortfall, generating under-recoveries.
  4. Accumulating shortfalls compress operating margins and cash flows of state refiners.

High volatile crude procurement costs drive up the landed cost of LPG for domestic oil companies. Because domestic cooking gas is sold at regulated price points below market parity, refiners cannot immediately pass increased costs onto consumers. Over a horizon of weeks to months, this shortfall manifests as under-recoveries on company balance sheets, reaching an estimated ₹370 per cylinder by December 2026. Ultimately, these unrecovered costs reduce quarterly profitability unless offset by government compensation.

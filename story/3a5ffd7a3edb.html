Government extends cotton import duty exemption until December 31

Textile mills gain temporary relief as imports head past 71 lakh bales.

  • Finance Ministry extended duty-free cotton imports until December 31, 2026.
  • Textile mills get cheaper raw cotton amid local crop quality and supply concerns.
  • Whether 2026-27 import volumes surpass previous record of 71 lakh bales.
Textile & ApparelAgricultureFiscal & BudgetTrade & Tariffs

What happened

The Finance Ministry has extended the customs duty exemption on cotton imports until December 31, 2026. The exemption was initially granted in May 2026 and was scheduled to expire on October 31. Industry trade sources estimate that cotton imports for 2026-27 could equal or exceed the 71 lakh bales imported in the previous year.

Who is affected

textile and apparel manufacturers
Gain access to duty-free foreign raw cotton to offset domestic supply issues.
domestic cotton farmers
Face increased competition from duty-free imported cotton during the harvest season.

Which sectors

  • Textile & Apparel tailwind — Duty-free imports help mills lower raw material input costs and overcome domestic supply deficits.
  • Agriculture headwind — Inflow of duty-free foreign cotton can limit domestic price appreciation for local growers.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: Government extends customs duty exemption on foreign raw cotton
  2. step 2: Reduces landed import costs for Indian textile spinning mills
  3. step 3: Maintains steady supply of raw cotton for apparel manufacturers
  4. step 4: Protects operating margins for textile companies facing domestic crop shortfalls

Extending the import duty exemption keeps landed foreign cotton cheaper for Indian spinning mills through the end of the calendar year. This enables textile units to supplement domestic crop shortages without incurring high tariff charges. Cheaper raw materials help garment manufacturers maintain output and fulfill export orders without suffering margin compression. Observers note these dynamics could influence financial results for textile companies over the coming quarters.

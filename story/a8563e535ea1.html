SEBI study finds 88% of retail F&O traders lost money in FY26

Options made up 92% of retail futures‑and‑options losses

  • 88% of retail F&O traders lost money in FY26.
  • High loss rates could trigger tighter regulator oversight.

What happened

On August 22, 2026, SEBI released a study covering the fiscal year 2025‑26 that found 88% of individual futures‑and‑options (F&O) traders incurred losses. The report said options accounted for 92% of the aggregate losses among retail participants, even as overall trading volumes fell.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. SEBI study reveals that 88% of retail F&O participants lost money, with options driving 92% of losses.
  2. Regulators may consider tightening margin requirements or position limits to protect investors.
  3. Retail traders would need to reduce option positions or exit the market, lowering turnover.
  4. Reduced turnover translates into lower brokerage fees and could depress earnings of brokerage and exchange operators.

The study’s loss figures act as a trigger for regulators, who then evaluate investor protection measures. Any new margin or position limits force traders to scale back or close option positions, which immediately cuts market turnover. The lag between rule‑making and trader response is a few weeks, after which brokers see reduced fee income reflected in quarterly earnings. Over months, the cumulative effect can reshape revenue streams for the whole financial‑services chain.

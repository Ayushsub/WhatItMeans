Oil supply shock and slower AI spending could pressure margins from q2fy27

Q2fy27 margin pressure targets small‑ and mid‑cap firms, says nuvama

  • Nuvama flags oil shock and fading AI capex as margin risks from q2fy27
  • Margin squeeze could curb earnings of cyclical and small‑mid cap firms
  • Watch oil prices, AI spending trends and q2fy27 corporate margins

What happened

On 5 september 2026 Nuvama Institutional Equities warned that an oil supply shock, fading domestic stimulus and a slowdown in AI capital expenditure could pressure corporate margins from the second quarter of fiscal year 2027 (q2fy27). The report highlighted small‑ and mid‑cap companies and other cyclical firms as most vulnerable. It also noted that the positive impact of AI investment on exports and metal prices may weaken in the second half of fy27.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. step 1: oil supply shock and slowdown in ai capex emerge
  2. step 2: higher energy costs and reduced productivity gains force firms to tighten margins
  3. step 3: firms either raise product prices or cut costs, affecting earnings and consumer spending
  4. step 4: earnings pressure appears in q2fy27 results and may lead to slower share‑price appreciation

The shock to oil supply raises fuel and logistics costs, while a slowdown in ai spending curtails efficiency gains. Together they squeeze operating margins, which firms may try to offset by passing costs to customers or cutting expenses. The effect shows up with a lag of a few quarters, first in corporate earnings reports and then in market valuations.

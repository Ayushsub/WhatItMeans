Nvidia earnings and Jackson Hole symposium to test global market rally

What happened

Global financial markets are bracing for two major events in the upcoming week: chipmaker Nvidia's quarterly earnings report and the US Federal Reserve's annual Jackson Hole symposium. These events arrive as a recent spike in global bond yields increases borrowing costs, putting downward pressure on equity valuations. Additionally, central bankers will gather to address persistent global inflation, which is being further complicated by soaring oil prices.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over weeks

  1. Central banks signal higher-for-longer interest rates at Jackson Hole
  2. Global bond yields rise further as investors demand higher returns
  3. Borrowing costs increase for multinational corporations and financial institutions
  4. Higher discount rates reduce the present value of future corporate earnings, depressing stock prices

When central bank chiefs hint at persistent inflation and high interest rates, bond yields rise globally as investors adjust their expectations. This increase in yields directly raises the cost of capital for companies, making new debt more expensive to service. For high-growth sectors like technology, higher discount rates are applied to future cash flows, which typically compresses their current stock valuations. This entire adjustment process usually plays out across global equity markets over several weeks as portfolios are rebalanced.

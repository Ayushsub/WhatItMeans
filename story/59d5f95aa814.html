Bank lending to commercial sector jumps 55% to 17.5 lakh cr in apr‑aug

Corporate bond issuances fell sharply as yields climbed in FY27

  • Bank credit surged 55.4% to 17.5 lakh cr Apr‑Aug FY27
  • Higher yields push firms to banks, shrinking bond market
  • Track bond issuance, RBI policy moves
banking & financialscorporate sectorCredit & Liquidity

What happened

During April‑August FY27, bank lending to the commercial sector rose 55.4% to about ₹17.5 lakh crore, while non‑food bank credit hit ₹9.8 lakh crore. At the same time, corporate bond fundraising fell sharply as yields on government securities rose.

Which sectors

  • banking & financials tailwind — strong loan demand lifts credit growth and fee income
  • corporate sector headwind — higher yields curb bond financing, raising reliance on costlier bank loans

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: rising yields on sovereign bonds raise cost of corporate bonds
  2. step 2: firms cut bond fundraising and turn to banks for financing
  3. step 3: banks expand loan book, increasing interest‑income streams
  4. step 4: earnings of banks improve while corporates face higher loan costs

Higher sovereign yields raise the benchmark cost of borrowing, forcing companies to abandon bond issuance. This forces them to seek bank credit, which banks meet by expanding their loan book. The extra credit shows up as higher interest income for banks in quarterly results, while corporate earnings may feel pressure from higher financing costs. The lag is a few weeks for bond market reaction and a month‑plus for loan disbursement to affect earnings.

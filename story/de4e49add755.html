ITAT Ahmedabad deletes ₹3.41 crore tax on fraudulent land sale

An Ahmedabad landowner was billed ₹3.41 crore for a transaction he never authorized.

  • ITAT Ahmedabad cleared a landowner of a ₹3.41 crore tax demand on a forged sale.
  • It establishes that tax cannot be levied on fraudulent transactions where no income was received.
  • How tax authorities handle similar cases of identity theft and forged property transactions.
Real Estate

What happened

An Ahmedabad landowner faced a ₹3.41 crore tax assessment after his co-owned land was fraudulently sold for ₹6.82 crore using forged signatures and a fake power of attorney. The landowner did not receive any proceeds from this unauthorized transaction. The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) Ahmedabad subsequently deleted the tax addition, ruling in favor of the landowner.

Who is affected

property owners facing fraudulent transactions
They gain a legal precedent protecting them from tax liabilities on income they never actually received.
income tax department assessors
They must verify the actual receipt of funds and transaction validity before raising tax demands on disputed sales.

Which sectors

  • Real Estate neutral — While it highlights the risks of land fraud, the ruling protects innocent landowners from unfair tax liabilities.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over quarters

  1. fraudsters forge signatures to execute an unauthorized land sale
  2. tax authorities register the transaction and raise a tax demand on the registered owner
  3. the victim appeals the tax assessment by proving the transaction was fraudulent
  4. the ITAT deletes the tax addition because no real income was received by the owner

When a fraudulent land sale is registered, the transaction data automatically triggers an income tax notice for capital gains. If the landowner can prove in court that their signature was forged and they received no money, the legal system intervenes. The ITAT Ahmedabad ruling confirms that tax is only leviable on real income, not on paper transactions arising from fraud. This process typically takes several quarters to resolve through appellate channels.

ITAT

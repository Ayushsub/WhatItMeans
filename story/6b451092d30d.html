Trinidad and Tobago seeks Indian oil firms to process Venezuelan crude

Two Indian majors eye a renovated Guaracara refinery for cheap Venezuelan oil

  • India's ONGC Videsh and Oil India discuss processing Venezuelan crude
  • Deal could lower feedstock costs if Guaracara refinery restarts
  • Watch refinery renovation progress and Venezuelan export policies
Energy & UtilitiesOil & Gas

What happened

On October 24, 2026, the government of Trinidad and Tobago announced a proposal for ONGC Videsh and Oil India to process crude from neighbouring Venezuela at the Guaracara refinery in Pointe‑à‑Pierre. The refinery, part of the former Petrotrin complex, is currently under renovation.

The transmission mechanism

Plays out over months

  1. step 1: Trinidad and Tobago proposes a processing agreement with ONGC Videsh and Oil India
  2. step 2: Indian firms secure access to Venezuelan crude, likely at discounted terms
  3. step 3: If the Guaracara refinery completes renovation, the crude can be processed, reducing feedstock costs
  4. step 4: Lower input costs translate into higher refining margins and may lower fuel prices for consumers

The announcement creates an immediate negotiation trigger, prompting Indian firms to line up procurement plans within weeks. The next stage depends on the Guaracara refinery’s refurbishment, which typically takes several months, before any crude can be processed. Once the plant is operational, the cheaper Venezuelan feedstock can lower input costs, improving refining margins that may eventually be reflected in consumer fuel prices. Each step introduces a lag: deal‑making (days), refinery completion (months), margin impact (months).
